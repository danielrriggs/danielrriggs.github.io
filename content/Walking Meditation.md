One of the easiest meditation practices that you can do.

Why walk as opposed to sit when we meditate?

One of the challenges in meditation is that if our body becomes a distraction or if our mind becomes a distraction, it is hard to meditate. When we sit in meditation and then our back starts aching or something starts cramping or our foot falls asleep, all of these will serve as distractions to our practice. This is part of the reason why we start meditation with the process of asana or yoga postures. We want to make our body fit and ready to sit for extended periods of time. If we are novice meditators, it can take a long time to be able to sit, for example, in Ardha Padmasana for an extended period of time.

The human body is naturally designed to walk for extended periods of time. So some of these physical distractions that we have when we sit and meditate are eliminated in walking meditation. There are also a couple of very interesting mental benefits.

As we sit, the mind is prone to wander. When you walk, the mental energy of your mind naturally starts to calm down. When it comes to trauma, the body can be a source of pain or suffering that we try to avoid. Centering or grounding ourself in the body is very easy with walking. It anchors our awareness in some capacity in the body. But since we aren't focusing on a particular sensation or trying to keep our mind on a particular part of our body, it's a natural and easy way to bring the awareness to the body without doing so in a shocking or intense manner.

###### Walking Meditation
1) Inhale for 4 counts
2) Exhale for 4 counts
3) 5 - 30 minutes to start

There are entire pilgrimages in the Himalayas where people will do walking meditations for hours at a time, but we'll start anywhere from five to 30 minutes, whatever suits you. The [[Zen]] tradition, will walk in a circle, but you can walk in a line, a circle, it really doesn't matter.

If you want to incorporate a walking meditation into a 30-minute window, walking falls in the first part, a 10 minute mind-body practice. Those who don't have access to something like a [[Yoga]] studio or a way to learn [[Tai Chi]], walking is a wonderful way to reconnect with your body.

If you enjoy an introspective meditation, an exploratory meditation, many of these can be combined with walking, because it's just simply a physical posture.

Whenever we do a meditative practice, the most important thing is that our spines should be straight, our neck should be straight, and our head should be straight. So even when we walk, try to stand up straight while you're walking.

Some experiences get coded as memories. When we put these together, we develop a sense of [[Nature of Self|self]].

Some experiences, instead of shaping our identity, get shaped **by** our identity. For example, a person with [[Trauma]] may think of themselves as not very smart. If they get an 'A', they may think it was because the professor likes them, or because they got lucky, not because they are smart. We can get locked into a sense of identity, especially when [[Trauma]] is at play.

Which experiences get coded as us? The brain is selective about which things form our identity. What determines whether I remember something or not? **Memories** have a couple of key features:
1) Recency
2) Salience
3) Emotional Importance
In the anatomy of the brain, the part that stores memories, the [[Hippocampus]], is right next to the part that experiences emotion, our [[Limbic system]], and there are a lot of reciprocal connections.

There are some exceptions to this, like in anger - we aren't recording memories to begin with, but generally speaking, the more emotional something is, the more likely it is to form a memory.

Various parts of brain do different things
[[Reticular Activating Formation|Reticular Activating System]] - governs our sleep-wake cycle.
[[Occipital Cortex]] - visual processing.
[[Auditory Cortex]] - audio processing.
[[Frontal lobes]] - gives us a sense of discipline & willpower.
[[Nucleus accumbens]] - gives us pleasure & [[Dopamine]].
[[Default Mode Network]] - stitches together our life experiences and memories and gives us our sense of [[Nature of Self|self]] - the conclusions we've formed about ourselves. Active when we are thinking about ourselves. Where our sense of narrative and [[Ego|identity]] comes from.

When the [[Default Mode Network|DMN]] becomes overactive, it can result in things like [[Depression]]. We want the [[Default Mode Network|DMN]] to be moderately active. The [[Mind]] of a depressed person is constantly thinking about themselves. The [[Depression|depressed]] person is very self critical. This is why [[Ketamine]], a dissociative agent, can be used for [[Depression]]. Inducing a dissociation shuts off the [[Default Mode Network|DMN]], causing a sense of relief, and can even be used as a treatment for [[Depression]].

[[Trauma]]s are emotionally charged. They can shape us.

We induce compensatory mechanisms, like numbing, but until that compensatory mechanism kicks in a traumatic experience is still very emotionally charged.

The stronger the stronger the memory. The stronger the memory, the bigger part of our [[Ego|identity]] it forms.

*I can get an A in 10 classes, but a single F is what I remember*.

The first bias we have with [[Trauma]] is that our brain will weight these powerful negative emotional experiences as a relatively larger part of our identity, because that's how we're wired.

This is also why people who grew up in [[Trauma|traumatic]] experiences of control, abuse, or neglect tend to have problems with their brain and can have problems like low [[Self-Image|Self-Esteem]].

[[Trauma]] can cause our self of [[Ego|identity]] to **freeze** and prevent us from moving forward. The mechanism: strong emotional experiences create memories which shape our sense of identity. Once I'm traumatized, I start to engage in [[Hemispheric Lateralization]], numbing behaviors like [[Addiction]]s, and I stop feeling things. If I stop feeling things, I no longer have emotionally relevant experiences and memories, which means that my [[Ego|identity]] can never change. I essentially become frozen in time. This is what the experience of traumatized people is - *I got stuck. I've been this way my whole life and haven't grown.* These people aren't capable of having new emotional experiences because they're numbing themselves out.

The work in trauma focused psychotherapy is often about having **corrective emotional experiences** to fix the emotions of the past by recalibrating in the psychotherapy experience.

Traumatic experiences are very damaging in childhood for two reasons:
- 1) The absence of healthy parenting can cause problems.
	- Healthy parenting helps children interpret and metabolize emotions, and can give corrective behaviors.
- 2) Children don't have a robust theory of [[Mind]].
	- Peekaboo is fun because children have no sense of object permanence. This is evolutionary. I am solely responsible for learning how to walk. It gives kids a relentless ability to keep practicing despite the fact that they fail over and over and over again.

Adults blame others for their problems - I didn't get promoted because my boss has a preference for other people and doesn't recognize my work, not because I didn't work well enough or that other people deserve to be more promoted than me.

 If other people don't exist and something bad happens to you, **you** are the one responsible for it. From a traumatic perspective, this is a huge problem because then I become responsible for all of the hurt that I feel. A young child that is physically abused or emotionally abused believes they are the ones responsible because they're not aware that other actors exist.

The earlier you get traumatized, the more impactful the trauma is.

It doesn't mean that you're this way forever. The human being is incredibly resilient, and humans' capacity to heal is just truly amazing.

A poor upbringing heavily influences our trajectory in life. It's when the majority of our identity formation happens and we lay down our basic sense of confidence. If we aren't given positive [[Behavioral reinforcement|Reinforcement]] from our parents, we enter adolescence with a low sense of [[Self-Image|Self-Esteem]].

When you have a problem with your identity, in older years, the first thing you see is a sense of living life very **reactively**, or, on **autopilot**. A person with a poorly formed identity doesn't **move towards things**. A lot of people feel like they aren't **going anywhere**. They're just existing from day to day. Having [[Goal]]s in life requires a sense of [[Nature of Self|self]]. The sense of self is what progresses in life. The future dimension of life requires a sense of [[Nature of Self|self]].

When our sense of [[Nature of Self|self]] is malformed or isn't very healthy, there's no point in planning for the future because I don't really **know who I am**. So we see a life that's very autopilot driven.

The other major impact of a traumatic identity has to do with **relationships**. When we have a poorly formed, unlovable sense of [[Nature of Self|self]], the way that we connect to other people changes. I may become a people pleaser or a doormat. If I don't know who I am, I can become a chameleon in relationships, adopting the other person's interests.

Forming a **new identity** requires the integration of emotional experiences. The first thing that we've got to do is **reconnect** with our emotions. We can't form our identity without new emotional experiences. This is a big challenge for people who've been traumatized because the whole reason we numb our emotions is because they are overwhelming, bad, and never ending.

You can reconnect with your emotions. Reconnecting with your emotions will automatically start the process of identity formation.

The next thing that we really need to start doing is **spending time with ourselves**. This too can be very difficult. Often, traumatized people can't tolerate boredom at all. When they sit with themselves, all the stuff they tried to keep numb comes roaring to the surface. They hate being themselves because they keep negative thoughts at bay through distraction. You need to spend time with yourself, without a device. You'll be able to tolerate it. Spending time in nature is a safe way to spend time with yourself.

There are other methods of forming your identity.

The Eastern karmic religions (Buddhists, Hindu, meditative traditions) had this idea that our sense of [[Nature of Self|self]] is not actually who we really are. We have a true [[Nature of Self|self]], which is that which experiences. It doesn't have a form or a sense of [[Ego|identity]]. The truest sense of self is something like the formless capacity for experience.

The one thing that separates you from everybody else is that you are the one who experiences your life. Your life can be filled with joy, sadness, excitement, pleasure, frustration, anger, whatever. It's kind of like you were a TV screen. On a given day, you have an experience, which is like a particular thing that happens on the screen. Every day what's on the screen changes. Thoughts come and go, emotions come and go, but the one thing that's always constant is the screen itself. What are the qualities of the screen? It doesn't have a particular color. It doesn't emit a particular sound. It is just a neutral space on which the experiences of life are projected. And that's truly what you are.

**As we connect** with this part of ourself, a lot of this **negative identity starts to melt away**. [[Meditation]] shuts off the [[Default Mode Network|DMN]] in the same way that [[Ketamine]] does. Psychedelics do the same thing. They dissolve our sense of self and allow us to feel this connection with other things. All of these experiences are possible through [[Meditation]]. As we go down the meditative route, we unravel our sense of identity.

Experiences are related to [[Samskara]]s. When we string together [[Samskara]]s, this is what our sense of [[Ego|identity]] is. Our ultimate antidote to this is completely moving past the concept of identity. When you inject some of these concepts from the East into psychotherapy protocols or evidence-based treatments, we see very good outcomes.

Some conditions, like [[Borderline Personality Disorder]], are very highly tied to a history of a [[Trauma|traumatic]] childhood. In [[Borderline Personality Disorder|BPD]], we don't have a stable sense of [[Nature of Self|self]]. The way that I feel about myself is determined by how people treat me. *If someone is happy with me, then I am a good person. If someone is angry with me, then I am a bad person.* The sense of identity is way too fluctuant and way too dependent on the external circumstances. When we teach people with [[Borderline Personality Disorder|BPD]].

**Fixing our identity**
1) **Understand** how the identity was formed. Understand that the conclusions that you made about yourself are by definition biased. Depending on how traumatic your upbringing is, remember that there's a **selection bias** where you will remember the most painful things
2) Reconnect with your emotions. When we're traumatized, we disconnect from our emotions. When we disconnect from our emotions, we can no longer shape our identity.
3) Transcendent methods ([[Meditation]], mindfulness stuff, spirituality) works on the [[Default Mode Network|DMN]] and can bypass this whole phenomenon of identity.

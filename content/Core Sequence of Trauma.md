Core Sequence of Trauma:
1. Live **reactively** due to control or neglect
2. Hyper-sensitive to signs of danger
3. Numb self to harsh emotions
4. Draw conclusions about the world
5. Shapes one's identity
6. Dictates one's relationships

The original research on trauma was first coined as "shell shock" after World War II. Doctors noticed that soldiers who had no apparent physical injuries had difficulty reintegrating into life. Problems holding down jobs, addictions, couldn't motivate themselves, difficulty in relationships, etc. Tests couldn't find anything wrong.

Through studies (on especially prisoners of war) we started to discover that someone in a traumatic environment would manifest problems. We started to map out the idea of [[Trauma]], or, [[Post Traumatic Stress Disorder|PTSD]]. Then, a group of researchers noticed that the patterns trauma which affected soldiers was visible in other places - in people who had never seen combat. One of the central studies for this was a study on Romanian orphanages. Orphans who were neglected. They manifested similar symptoms as soldiers from WWI and WWII.

As the picture became clearer and clearer, we started to see it in lots of different places - in kids who had single parents, or kids who had parents who were alcoholics or had drug problems, or even kids who moved around a lot. As we started to understand what the picture of trauma looked like, we realized that "normal" people seemed to have some of these manifestations. Now, when we look at a lot of the common problems that people face today - difficulty in relationships, a lack of motivation, a lack of self-confidence, it can all be mapped to this core sequence of trauma.

###### 1) Live **reactively** due to control or neglect
What does the sequence look like? It starts with an environment of **coercive control** or **neglect**. In the environment of coercive control (this is more based on prisoners), there's a human being who is not responsible for the direction of their life. Their whole life is dictated by someone else. *Whether I live or die today, whether I get to eat today or I go hungry today, all depends on the guards*. We start to live life very reactively. What I want doesn't really matter because I have no control in this situation. These prisoners became hypersensitive to the reactions of the guards.

We even see this in kids with tiger parents. These are parents who don't care about what you want - Who cares about hopes and dreams? We need you to be a doctor, lawyer, investment banker. You need to be the perfect kid. And we don't care what you want. Your life is dictated by us. The common element here is an environment of coercive control that you have to really react to constantly. What you do today depends on this omnipotent force outside of you.

This **controlling force** doesn't have to be physical in nature. It can be psychological or emotional manipulation because you're being controlled by the emotions of someone around you. Or it can be economic or financial. As we learn more about trauma, we started to realize that there are more and more situations that can trigger this sequence. The end result, though, is the same - what matters internally matters way less than how someone else feels.

###### 2) Hyper-sensitive to signs of danger
As we start to live reactively, it changes the way that our physiology is wired. Our arousal system, our danger detection system, becomes very, very hypersensitive. A prisoner in a prison camp is hypersensitive to the moods of the guard. The same thing is true of a child who grows up with an alcoholic parent. This has physiologic effects. Our body detects danger more easily, creates emotions like [[Anxiety]] more easily, increases our adrenaline, increases our cortisol.

Once we can detect danger very easily, our internal emotional state becomes way worse. Because now I'm seeing danger anywhere. Even in situations that should make me feel safe and relaxed, I'm feeling anxious and fearful. There are some studies that show that people who grow up in traumatic situations can detect even small amounts of dissatisfaction in the faces of loved ones.
*If I'm in an argument with my partner and my partner comes back and says, hey, I'm sorry we got into an argument. I'm all better. But even if they're 10% resentful, if they're 10% upset with me, I will literally ignore the 90% satisfied that my partner is and home in on the 10% because I've been wired to detect danger.*
This results in an overwhelmingly negative emotional experience of life. Once my life is filled with fear and anxiety, how do I cope with that?

###### 3 Numb self to harsh emotions
Now we get to the second thing that happens in traumatized individuals - numbness and dissociation. If I'm feeling [[Anxiety|anxious]] or fearful all the time, to adapt, my brain will numb myself out. Because I can't fix the situation. I can't make my alcoholic parent like me. And I certainly can't make them stop drinking. So, I'm just going to mentally unplug.

What starts as thought suppression leads to actual emotional numbness (I won't even feel the fear anymore). In the most severe cases, this can manifest as full-blown dissociation.

If I'm in a constant state of stress and I'm feeling fear and anxiety all the time, this shapes the way that I live my life. It shapes their cognitions. Difficulty studying, sleeping.

###### 4) Draw conclusions about the world
Then I start to form conclusions about life - *Life is painful. I'm not good at life. Life is filled with hardship. Regular people are motivated and happy, but I'm not allowed to have that kind of life.*

###### 5) Shapes one's identity
Once we start to think about the world in a lot of negative ways, that negatively shapes our identity.

Here's how this works:
*Let's say I'm emotionally numb, stressed & anxious all the time. Now I did poorly on this test. Since I'm emotionally numb and I'm very anxious, it's hard for me to make friends. And since I'm full of fear, it's hard for me to have romantic relationships. I suck at tests and academics. I suck at making friends. And I suck at romantic relationships. What is the only conclusion that is left I suck as a human being?*

###### 6) Dictates one's relationships
Once I conclude that I'm a bad human being, how am I supposed to be happy in a relationship?

Now that we have a roadmap of the core sequence of trauma, we also have an idea of what we need to fix. It's a cascade of adaptations. We need to un-adapt in each of those ways. We'll start with physiologic rewiring.

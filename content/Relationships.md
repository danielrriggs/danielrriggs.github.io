 If you grew up in a household where there was abuse, neglect, or things were extremely controlling, that that will shape the way that you see people and the way that you form connections. Or, even if you didn't grow up in an abusive household, something about the way that your parents related to them or were cold or couldn't handle some of your emotions then lingers into adulthood, and you can have difficulty forming connections.

Powerful forces can keep you stuck in patterns, staying in an abusive or a one-way relationship. This can be very perplexing to people on the outside. Or, we can be clingy, and partners can tell us they need space which triggers [[Anxiety]], fear, and abandonment.

One of the hallmarks of [[Trauma]] in relationships is that we tend to go through the same cycle over and over again. Despite the fact that we change partners or try to change our attitude or explore new areas to meet people we tend to find ourselves repeating the same patterns in relationships.

**Principles through which human beings connect** ([[Attachment Theory]])
**Mirroring** - your ability to understand your internal state depends on the environment around you. Our understanding of our internal emotional state depends on the emotional expression of the people around us.

Most people think that there's me on the inside and then there's separate stuff on the outside. But it turns out that when children grow up in supportive environments, their understanding of their internal emotional state is way more robust.

A lot of [[Awareness]] of our internal emotional state, as well as the ability we have to regulate our internal emotional state, depends on how much we were mirrored in childhood.

*Example: when a kid falls and everyone laughs, the kid will get up and laugh. If a kid falls and everyone around panics, the kid will get up and cry.*

A kid doesn't know what they feel. They don't have the awareness of the subtlety of their emotional state. They can't distinguish between anger, sadness, etc; they only know that they're content or discontent. They learn by paying attention to what people around them are doing. Adults in any society will have an exaggerated emotional expression when dealing with kids.

When children are not emotionally mirrored, they do not develop an understanding of what goes on inside them - they develop [[Alexithymia]].

Most men **normative male alexithymia** because the way we raise boys discourages emotional expression and dads tend to not offer exaggerated mirroring responses towards boys. The less of that we have, the more color blindness we develop internally.

This isn't necessarily abuse or neglect. If what you felt was not mirrored or received appropriately by your caregivers, there's a chance that you don't even know exactly what you're feeling because you don't have the vocabulary for it. This can be so confusing as you move through life because you're not quite sure why you are behaving the way that you're behaving. You're not sure why you can't get your [[Anxiety]] under control and you have the inability to regulate it.

When our parents or caregivers mirror emotional responses to us, not only does it teach us what we feel, this is also how we **regulate** what we feel. Adults are an emotional vessel to a child. Also, as an adult, you can vent to friends and they can carry some of that emotional weight.

Nowhere in medical school did I ever learn that kissing a boo-boo makes it better. It offloads the emotional energy and the child feels comforted. It's psychological.

 If you had an emotionally stable environment, supportive environment (which means that people acknowledged, mirrored, and received your emotions), you will gain two advantages in life:
 1) You will **know** what you feel
 2) You will be able to **regulate** your feelings

If you grew up in an environment where you did not have those things, the **good news** is that **you are not screwed**. Our brains are plastic and we can learn new things.

In [[Psychotherapy]] for [[Trauma]], a big part of what we do is help people identify their feelings and regulate them. That's why a psychotherapist will constantly ask you, "how does that make you feel?"

Emotional mirroring leads to emotional awareness and emotional regulation.

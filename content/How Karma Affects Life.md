[[Trauma]] creates ripple effects effects across all of these dimensions of being. It rewires our physiology, affects our emotions, affects the way that we think, affects the way that we perceive ourselves, affects our relationships and the way that we connect with other people. It can feel overwhelming.

Think about your [[Mind]] as a garden. How we tend it determines what grows. If our physiology is wired in a certain way, let's say to be hyper-aroused or easily arousable, this is a weed that needs to be removed.

If we want to cultivate something, let's say friendship, it requires a constant attention to a particular part of our garden.

Young saplings are tender and fragile. As they receive some effort and grow, they become more robust and begin to bear fruit. Friendship can turn into a romance which can turn into a marriage which can turn into an entire life. Now we have a powerful resilient tree. If something bad happens in other parts of my garden, this part of the garden which used to require a lot of work is now bearing fruit in season.

It doesn't matter how overrun your garden is. The same actions can be taken to get to a healthy garden.

If you think that some people have advantages and inherited a great garden - the worst case of [[Addiction]] I've ever seen came from your classic trust fund baby. A lot of times what brings people out of [[Addiction]] is a limitation of resources. When you've hit rock bottom, you're homeless, you don't have a job, people divorce you, you have nothing left in your life because your [[Addiction]] burnt up all your resources. This trust fund baby with an almost infinite amount of money, no matter how hard they used drugs, always had money to throw to other people and will never be homeless. This person was financing whole drug dealers careers because of how much money they had. The worst case of [[Addiction]] comes from the best circumstances.

Be careful about jumping to conclusions about what your situation is like or even assuming that other people have it better. I'm not saying that other people don't have it better; the point is that even if you inherited a beautiful orchard, if you give into your mental impulses, it can be overrun by weeds.

1) **Physiology**
Our mind always wonders, *"when am I gonna do it? When am I done? How much do I have to do to fix this problem?"* The moment that we start thinking about a [[Goal]] orientation in that way, the mind also slips in with other kinds of BS. *"Why do I have to work for 40 days, and this person only has to work for 10 days?"* We need to be careful about this way of thinking. I don't know how long fixing my problem will take. All I know is that every day that you do the right action is one weed that's been pulled from your garden.

**If you take the right actions toward your life and body, the right things will happen.** Even drastic levels of change are possible if we take the right actions.

2) **Emotions**
This is an area we tend to actively neglect; we sow a lot of bad [[Karma]]s. When there's so much negative emotion, we turn back to some substance or numbing technique -and our brain is gonna help us with [[Hemispheric Lateralization]], by the way. As we retreat from our emotions, things get worse over time.

Therapy is one of the most intensive, fastest things to help you get weeding.

It's true that [[Trauma]] causes problems, but each of these problems is propagated by our actions today. If I had a screwed up childhood, the actions I take today will propagate the pattern that was built in the past.

[[Trauma]] tricks us into thinking that things are permanent and that the actions that we take are useless. The tricky thing is that we tend to give up even when we're doing all of the right things. Take things slowly. Have [[Sraddha|Faith]] that if you take the right action over and over and over again, it is a principle of science that something good will happen.

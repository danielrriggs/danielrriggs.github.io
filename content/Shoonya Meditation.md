Shoonya is the Sanskrit word for void, emptiness, or even the concept of zero. It doesn't get easily translated.

Most of our life, we experience ourselves as a something. That something can be a good something or a bad something. But when it comes to emptiness or the void, there's nothing there, so it can be neither good nor bad.

There is a whole subset of practices in the meditative tradition that try to connect to the void or the nothingness within you. While this can be a little bit scary to think about, it can be absolutely powerful and transformative to actually engage in. I wouldn't recommend the Shoonya practice if you are actively struggling with a mental illness. Become stable before you do this.

This is also a practice that requires some degree of meditative experience or expertise before we engage it.

**The Practice**
Put your arms in a circle. Look down in between your arms and notice the space inside your arms. Unfocus your eyes and just notice the emptiness within your arms. Spend some time feeling that emptiness. Close your eyes. Feel the empty space. A wad of emptiness. It almost has some degree of substance to it. And after we spend some time feeling that emptiness, then we can finally relax the arms. But as we relax the arms, we want to hold our attention to the wad of emptiness that we felt. Or, open ourselves up to whatever we experience once we put our arms down.

Alias: Impulse, impulsive

The pattern of impulsive behavior, the inability to stick to a particular goal, or even [[Resistance|self-sabotage]] can be tracked back to [[Trauma]].

In the case of abusive, neglectful, or controlling environments, the common theme is that I am not in control of my circumstances. When a child can't control their circumstances, the only thing they can control is their own emotional state.

We can feel guilty, telling ourselves things like, *"my parents did the best they could, my upbringing was fine"*, but that doesn't change the circumstances. Our own guilt or shame for blaming our parents can get in the way of us seeing the developmental patterns of abuse, neglect, or control.

In a normal environment, if something makes me unhappy, I have the power to change that thing and change the way my environment affects me. Because I can't fix my circumstances, I'm left with just fixing my own internal state. This is called **emotion-focused coping**.

As adults, [[Emotion-Focused Coping]] is a good tool to have in our arsenal. If I had a rough week at work, I'm going to just try to make myself feel better. Totally fine. Kids, teenagers, and even young adults can rely too much on [[Emotion-Focused Coping]]. If we can't engage in it in healthy ways, we tend to numb ourselves out. We see this in the case of [[Hemispheric Lateralization]]. Our brain is decreasing activity across the corpus callosum to create an emotional numbness.

We also learn other kinds of coping mechanisms that alter our emotions, like video games. As long as I'm playing a video game, my emotions are far away. For other people, it's other sources of numbing - social media, drugs. All of these interventions are designed to make me feel better and not fix the problem.

The more that we focus on [[Emotion-Focused Coping]], the more impulsive we will become.

What is an impulse? It's a feeling, like wanting to do something. It arises suddenly and drives us toward a particular behavior. In the [[Mind]], impulses are more like emotions than analytical reasoning - they come and they go. We can have the impulse first and then we [[Rationalization|Rationalize]] the impulse. Impulses and emotions are closely related. They're almost the same thing.

As I numb my emotions, it isn't that they stop acting - they manifest in unusual ways.

What creates the impulse to move across the country spontaneously? Some amount of dissatisfaction with where you live now.
- *I don't feel good in this area. I feel sad. I feel like I'm not progressing. I feel stuck in some way.*
These are all emotional experiences. Because we utilize [[Emotion-Focused Coping]] and have [[Hemispheric Lateralization]], we don't feel the stuckness. Instead, that stuckness burbles to the surface and looks like an emotion to move spontaneously across the country.
- *Because, once I move across the country, then I will feel better. It'll be a whole new environment and everything is going to be different over there. A new relationship will fix me. Joining the military will fix me.*
The problem is that [[Wherever you go, there you are]]. We can get into cycles of leaving things half abandoned. Changing majors, jobs, relationships. Because we are always at the mercy of our impulses. Those impulses are driven by emotions, but we aren't even aware that emotions are controlling our life because of [[Hemispheric Lateralization]].

We see the same picture at play in [[Resistance|self-sabotage]]. For a person with self-esteem problems,
The more invested in a relationship they are, the more afraid they are that will screw it up. Screwing up a relationship that I don't care about is not a big deal. But, if I really love this person and can make a future with them, it hurts so much more. So, as the relationship progresses in the right direction, the [[Mind]] sabotages it to prevent screwing up something that matters to us even more. It tracks back to sub-surface emotions we aren't aware of.

There are a couple clues to sub-surface emotions:
- You will have an **impulse**, and in **close proximity** to that, there will be some kind of **emotionally numbing** thing - video games, drugs, social media, etc.
- **When** you engage in more emotionally numbing behavior
Why is it that you feel like playing video games on one day, but you don't feel like playing video games on another day? It's because your brain has learned when there are these sub-surface emotions that are getting out of control - this is the antidote to them.

So, what do we do about impulsivity and [[Resistance|self-sabotage]]? It's not just about restraint, although we do want restraint. The first key is to recognize that these impulses are being driven by an internal emotional experience. As you work on your emotions **through your body** and **increase emotional awareness**, some of this impulsivity and self-sabotage will actually start to decrease on its own.

Tips to help with impulsivity:
1) Recognize the triggers
When you feel an impulse, it's not random. It feels random, but that's because we're blind to what's driving it.
Ex: *What makes me waste time doomscrolling?* Usually, it's an emotional experience you're trying to avoid.
2) Develop alternate emotional regulation techniques.
Once you discover the trigger, explore it. Journal. Take a walk. Figure out how you feel about the trigger. As you explore these things, you'll notice all kinds of emotions come up. Use whatever emotional regulation technique works for you - talking to a therapist, commiserating with a friend, going for a walk, spending time with your dog, meditating, doing [[Yoga]], journaling. Just manage that emotion in some kind of way.
3) Set & enforce boundaries
This is arguably the most important. We learned the strategy of [[Emotion-Focused Coping]] because growing up, we could not actually fix our circumstances. As an adult, you actually have a lot of options when it comes to changing your circumstances.

Example:
You doom scroll because you're avoiding calling your emotionally manipulative parents to tell them that you can't come home for Thanksgiving because they'll guilt trip you into feeling bad.
Tell your parents *"I'm not coming home for Thanksgiving. I need you to not emotionally guilt trip me into it. I need you to manage your emotions without dumping them on me".*
You can just literally say that to them, and you can say that to your parents. Now, they're not gonna be able to do it. And that's when you extract yourself from the conversation. You can say, *"hey, I just can't handle this conversation right now. I recognize that this decision is disappointing to you, but I'm gonna go ahead and hang up. I'll talk to y'all in about five days. If there is some kind of life-threatening emergency, feel free to give me a call or text me. But otherwise, I'm not gonna answer the phone. I just can't deal with this right now."*

As an adult, it is very possible for you to set limits with people, enforce boundaries, let people know how you feel, or even change your circumstances. You can quit your job. You can find a new one. It doesn't have to be impulsive. You can do it through thought and planning.

**You can effect change around you.** You can change the circumstances that create your emotional state.

How do we know whether the desire to change a circumstance is part of the solution or whether it's actually giving into our impulse?
Let's go back to the nature of an impulse
- If it **fluctuates** over time very rapidly, chances are it's an impulse.
Emotions also fluctuate over time, but if it's a persistent experience over time, it's less likely to be an impulse.
*Lean into the things that you feel more strongly about over time in a relatively consistent way.*

**You are far more empowered now than you used to be.** Your [[Mind]] may tell you otherwise, but we'll get to that when we talk about [[Ego|identity]] and [[Personality]] and things like that.

A very common problem that people deal with is that their **life seems to have no sense of direction**. They're impulsive, going whatever direction life blows. There's no sense of forward direction in life. They're constantly struggling with impulsivity at the least or [[Resistance|self-sabotage]] at the worst. You find yourself in these cycles where you can't make any forward progress.

Both of these things are consequences of suppressing our emotions because self-sabotage and impulsivity are both driven by subsurface emotions.

As we connect with our emotions as we regulate our emotions, as we recognize our triggers, develop alternate ways to deal with our emotional state, and finally start to shape our circumstances because we're not five-year-old kids anymore, this is when the impulsivity and self-sabotage will start to change.

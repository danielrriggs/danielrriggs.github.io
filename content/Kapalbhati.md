Alias: Kapal Bhati.

(Breath of Fire)

[[Kapal]] is the Sanskrit word for 'skull' and [[Bhati]] is the Sanskrit word for 'illumination'.

Useful for people with [[Anxiety]]. It's a period of hyperventilation, and as we finish the practice, we'll be in a calm state.
- In a state of [[Anxiety]], the mind is going really, really fast; oftentimes we try to force ourselves to calm down, but when you try to force your [[Mind]] to do something, it usually doesn't listen (because that isn't how the [[Mind]] works). So instead, what we're going to do is match the pace of the [[Mind]].

Since we're very, very excited, we're going to breathe very, very fast, which is normally the rate of anxious breathing anyway; we're going to actually tire the [[Mind]] out by breathing way too fast. And then we'll sort of calm it down and naturally breathe very slowly.

###### Respiratory Physiology
Kapal Bhati has to do with respiratory physiology; the yogis figured out how to take advantage of our respiratory circuitry to induce calm breathing. Our respiratory drive is actually driven not by our oxygen level, like most people may think, but actually by our CO2 level. When our CO2 gets very, very high, we induce hyperventilation. When our CO2 gets very, very low, we actually slow down breathing. The main control of our breathing isn't actually oxygen. It's about blowing off the CO2 waste products that are in our body. The cool thing is that when we hyperventilate, we actually rapidly decrease our CO2. And then there's a very powerful driver within our body to naturally slow down your breath. When we naturally slow down our breath, our [[Mind]] is going to slow down with it.

###### Prerequisite: Abdominal breathing
Before we start with Kapal Bhati itself, we have to learn abdominal breathing.

- Sit in [[Meditation Posture]]. Close your eyes. Put your hand on your belly.
- Inhale into the belly, not into the chest. Feel the belly expand. As you exhale, contract the abdomen & pull it in.
- Close your eyes and do this for 5 breaths.
- Once done, notice how calm you are.

###### Kapalbhati
We're going to do a **forcible** abdominal **exhalation** at a very rapid pace, followed by passive inhalation.
We want to practice for a certain number of rounds; not a certain amount of time.

- We'll start with 9 rounds, then rest.
	- After the 9 rounds, notice the calm, slow natural inhalation. After a round, there will be a deep inhalation where you feel very calm & good.
	- **Slowly work your way up to 12, 15, 21, and eventually 33 breaths over the course of 2 weeks.**

The full practice will be 3 sets of 33 rounds.

It's fine to let go of the count completely and just focus on the practice; the counts are just for people who need a rule book. You can let yourself go within the practice and let your own respiratory centers guide you when you're ready for that long inhalation afterward.

 It's encouraged to, especially after that last set - to just sit silently and do whatever kind of other meditative practice you want to. You can do any of the other practices.
 
 The goal of Kapal Bhati, remember, is to kind of calm your mind. It works really well for [[Anxiety]] and really puts your mind in a good state for further or deeper practices of meditation.

We experience negative emotions, like [[Anxiety]] to protect us. Crying is a signal to the people around us that we need help. A child who grew up in a neglectful or abusive household can get punished for expressing these emotions. Instead of them resulting in help, they result in more problems.

As an adult, we have coping mechanisms.
- Cognitively reframe
	- *A breakup is something I can grow from*
- [[Meditation]]
- Support systems for negative emotion
Children without coping mechanisms unplug the whole emotional system - [[Hemispheric Lateralization]].

This process starts with emotional repression. Eventually, we become numb. The body shuts off emotions. In the most sever cases, it can result in full blown dissociation - where we completely unplug from our emotional experience of the moment.

In [[Hemispheric Lateralization]], we see decreases activity across the [[Corpus Callosum]]. [[Trauma]] survivors feel numb and can talk about their traumatic experiences in a detached way. That's an adaptation to protect ourselves. It protects us in the moment, but once we start the process of hemispheric lateralization, it starts to cause problems.

Emotions help us in a lot of ways. Let's say you walk into a room and you feel tension in the air. Your brain processes this very quickly. You notice that something is wrong. You don't really know what's wrong. You don't know who's upset. You don't know any details. But you feel [[Anxiety|anxious]]. Emotions act very rapidly to coordinate our response as a human being. Emotions are a very useful **source of information**. You may feel something's a bit shady. Or, you may be angry and not know why. Your brain is processing a lot of information and will feed it to you as an emotion. Once we numb ourselves from our emotions, we **stop getting information** about what's going on around us.

The second thing that emotions do is they're a very **powerful source of motivation**. They give us energy to act. If someone slaps you and you get angry, that energy allows you to protect your boundaries to keep our body and territory intact. It primes us to fight. If I'm numb to anger, it makes it easy for me to become a victim of abuse. This happens in [[Trauma]]. People with a history of abuse in childhood end up in abusive relationships in adulthood. If a child gets angry and is punished for it, they learn that emotions serve no purpose. They numb out and unplug their anger. When they grow to an adult, that circuit is still unplugged. They don't have the emotional energy to set limits and enforce boundaries.

Emotions give us information and provide us with motivation, but when we unplug from emotions, we run into problems:
- We can't articulate our emotions
	- *To articulate an emotion, we need to feel something in the right hemisphere, it crosses the [[Corpus Callosum]], and we articulate it with the left hemisphere.*
In order to articulate emotions, we need to have a sense of where we are and where we are going.

Not talking about emotions is a problem. Freud observed that language could substitute for action. When patients with psychosomatic neuroses talked about their problems with emotional energy, their problems got better. We aren't sure how this works, but articulating your emotions seems to calm them down and improves things in some way.

The inability to articulate emotions creates relationship problems - friendship, romantic, and professional. Being able to articulate and understand what you are feeling helps you harmonize with the people around you.

In spiritual traditions, such as [[Mantrasastra]], the spoken word can accumulate energy. There's something powerful about speaking words and healing and growth.

In the [[Flow State]], there's synchronicity across all regions of the brain. If we want to create a flow state, we need all parts of the brain working together. Your analytical mind is active and focused, but it's also combined with positive emotion. If a task is too easy, we feel bored (which is an emotion). If a task is too hard, we feel overwhelmed. For optimal performance, you need to be emotionally excited about the task. With [[Hemispheric Lateralization]], we see the exact opposite. People who don't have a unity between the right and left hemispheres either feel overly analytical or their emotions get the best of them and they can't [[Dharana|focus]].

Just because we don't feel feel our emotions doesn't mean they aren't active. There's a difference between numbness and absence.

The last problem with hemispheric lateralization is that all of our suppressed emotions leak out in other ways & can wreak havoc. They manifest as things like [[Addiction]], impulsivity, lack of [[Dharana|focus]] in life, or [[Somatic Illness]]. Emotions are very powerful [[Motivation|motivators]]. Emotion controls behavior.

One of the biggest problems with [[Trauma]] is that we insulate ourselves from our emotions. At the time we do it, this is an adaptive mechanism which helps us survive. We quit the whole game of emotions because it just brings pain. When we suppress emotions, the emotional circuitry in our brains doesn't stop working; we just become unaware of it.

To fix this, we need to reverse the process. This starts with **articulating** emotions. When we start to articulate emotions, they start to become **metabolized**. It allows us to move on. Articulating emotion isn't just good for the sake of **healing**, but even for the sake of **awareness**. If you don't know what emotions are, you're just a pinball getting knocked around by life - you have no idea why you're doing the things you're doing.

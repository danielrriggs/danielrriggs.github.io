## **Weekly Quest**

Karma is the principle that all causes have effects, and all effects can be traced back to causes. 

This quest focuses on mental karma, the idea that the mental processes that our mind engages in have consequences as well.

**Objective:** To explore and understand the nature of mental actions and their karmic imprint, learn to identify and detach from harmful mental attachments to minimize karmic accumulation.

#### **Lite Quests: To observe the mind's actions and their immediate effects.**

**Daily Reflection Meditation**

Mental karma involves the actions we take in our mind, such as thoughts, intentions, and desires. These mental actions can create patterns that influence our behavior and life experiences.

**Observe the mind’s activities. What thoughts did you have? What did these thoughts reinforce? How did they translate into actions or further thoughts?**

**Attachment Mapping** 

Every mental action has a result that can reinforce existing patterns (vasanas) and lead to further thoughts and actions. Mapping thoughts to identify recurring patterns and understand how they lead to specific actions or emotional states can help us recognize vasanas. 

List recurring thoughts from your daily thought log. What patterns do you notice? Are there things or outcomes you are strongly attached to? How do these attachments affect your emotions and decisions? Identify recurring thought patterns and their emotional ties, analyzing how they form chains of habitual tendencies (vasanas).

#### **Medium Quest: To understand how desires and attachments form and influence mental karma.** 

**Experience Without Attachment**  
Attachments are strong emotional ties we create in our mind, often leading to expectations and potential suffering. Expecting specific outcomes from our actions can lead to disappointment, a form of mental karma. Engaging in experiences without attachment allows us to enjoy the present moment without creating new mental karma.

Choose an activity you enjoy and engage in it fully without attachment to the outcome. Note your experience and how it affects your mental state and karma.

**Present Moment Living**  
Living fully in the present moment helps avoid the accumulation of karma, as it keeps us focused on current actions rather than future results or past regrets.

Commit to one day where you intentionally live in the present, being fully involved in every action. Let thoughts arise, and let them go. Observe the effect of this on your thoughts that day.

#### **Advanced Quest: To practice living in the present and understand its effects on karmic accumulation.** 

**Involvement and Karma**  
*Trying* to achieve and accomplish can lead to karma accumulation, while being fully present and involved in activities can prevent those activities from leaving a karmic footprint.

For a week, monitor your intentions behind each action. Are you intending more and accumulating karma, or are you being fully present and not accumulating karma? Reflect on the differences in your experiences.

For one week, track your daily intentions and their effects on your actions and mental state. Try to be as mindfully involved as possible. Notice the effect of this on the push and pull that your mind generates on you.

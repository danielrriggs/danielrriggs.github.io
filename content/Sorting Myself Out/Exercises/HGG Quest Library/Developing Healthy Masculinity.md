Over the next week, set aside time on three different days to do the given exercises. If you don’t identify as male, then you can think of a male-identifying person in your life, and do this exercise with them, or you can do it with yourself in mind.

Write down your thoughts on paper, a notes app, or another writing medium of choice. You can do it all in one sitting if you want, or over 2-3 days. It’s best to be in a calm state of mind before attempting this.

The point of this exercise is so that you are one step closer to living a life that is aligned with what you want. Men are often stuck between living up to other's expectations, and as you learn more about yourself on a deeper level, you gain a sense of agency and become more aware of the choices you have.

**Exercise 1: Understanding Your Masculinity**

* Try to answer these questions to the best of your ability:  
  * What does it mean to you to be a man?  
  * What does society expect from men?  
  * Which aspects of your masculinity, or your perception of masculinity, are born from negative experiences and emotions?  
    * **Tip: One way to identify insecurities is to catch yourself feeling like you want to be something else.**  
  * Which aspects of your masculinity come from a place of tranquility?

**Exercise 2: Gaining Tranquility**

* Engage in solo activity in nature for 1-2 hours. It could be a walk, a hike, or any other physical activity in nature. Avoid the use of technology to occupy your mind. Carry some paper with you, or find a way to access and answer the questions below.  
* After you’re done, sit quietly somewhere, with your eyes closed, and feel the “you-ness” of you. Just sit and let your awareness increase. Experience yourself existing.  
* Once you’re tranquil, you can take a few steps towards societal masculinity – but once again, things that **you choose to aspire to**, vs. the conditioning that you have acquired.  
* Try to recognize whether you are choosing an expectation or running away from the shame of not fulfilling a conditioned one. A great way of doing that is to think about meeting the expectation.  
  * Once you have met it, would you feel “normal”? Would you feel relieved? In that case, shame is likely the root of that expectation.  
  * Would you feel proud of accomplishing it? Would you feel good about yourself? In that case, it’s likely that it’s an expectation that you value, and have consciously adopted.  
* List aspects of masculinity and then determine whether the absence is shame.  
* List aspects of masculinity where you are “ok” with the absence, but would be proud of the achievement.  
* As you explore the “you-ness” of you. Ask yourself this: what aspects of this person are masculine?  
  * Feel them to the best of your ability. List them out if you’d like.

Remember to do these exercises from a tranquil place of mind. Avoid doing these exercises when you are emotionally charged.

You don't need to share these reflections with anyone, but they can be valuable for personal growth. If you're involved in any form of personal coaching, such as HG Personal or Group Coaching, consider discussing your insights with your coach or group.

## **Outline:**

- Self-esteem, or identity, is essential for personal development, as it helps us navigate and specialize within the social hierarchy.  
- Understand how self-esteem is formed at the neurological level, within social contexts, and according to the Vedic model.  
- We develop both positive and negative self-esteem to gauge the likelihood of our success in various situations.  
  - Our inherent inclination toward certainty often leads to the development of a negative self-esteem, which can help us survive challenging circumstances  
  - Low self-esteem often leads to excuses for avoiding responsibilities.  
- Learn practical methods to change our self-esteem and improve our self-perception, particularly those that involve shaking up our surroundings, emotions, or ego.

### Medium Quest – Mental Polarity Exercise

*The purpose of this exercise is to observe how you react to different statements about yourself. This will give you insight into where your sense of self comes from and how your mind shapes your sense of self and experiences.*

**Activity**– fill out the table below with the following steps:

* **Identify 3 neutral statements** about yourself. It can be about your abilities, personality traits, or experiences.  
* **Reframe each negatively and positively**, as shown in the examples below.  
* **Observe your reaction** to each new version. Notice how your emotions shift and what stories your mind tells you.  
* **Ask yourself: Are you more inclined to believe the negative or positive statement?** Which feels more true? Why?  
* Reflect on these concepts as **mental constructs**—labels that exist only in your mind. They aren’t objective truths about who you are.  
* **Step into the observing self**. Recognize that you can observe your thoughts and reshape your self-perception. This will have an effect on your self-esteem.

### Advanced Quest \- Do the Impossible\! Create New Emotional Experiences for New Self Esteem

*The purpose of this exercise is to challenge your mental patterns by creating new emotional experiences. Doing things you don’t believe you can do can introduce new emotions into your experience, shaking up your perception of self and allowing you to expand your self-esteem.*

**Activity–** fill out the table below with the following steps:

1. Choose an activity or two that you doubt you can do—something that feels beyond your abilities.  
2. **Complete the “impossible” activity and pay attention to your experience during and after.**  
3. **Reflect on your thoughts**:   
   1. Did you make excuses or downplay your success?  
   2. Compare how your mind interprets your own success vs. others' success. Do you give credit to others easily while diminishing your own achievements?

## **Outline:**

* The conscious and unconscious mind have an interactive relationship, with the unconscious floating things to the conscious and also restricting it (via suppression, psychological defense mechanisms, etc.)  
  * The *conscious* mind is what we are aware of – the active thoughts and emotions and other things that we are experiencing in the present.   
  * The *unconscious* is the storehouse of all the stuff that is not active (e.g., calculations, sensory input, memories) and creates scripts and interpretations of the world  
    * Salience is key – if you don’t feel something is important, your brain won’t subconsciously learn it.  
* Models of consciousness:  
  * Jungian model of consciousness:  
    * *Persona:* what we project  
    * *Conscious:* what’s underneath  
    * Individual and Collective Unconscious  
  * Four quadrants of consciousness (the weird stuff\!):  
    * Waking state  
    * Dreaming  
    * Deep sleep  
    * Samadhi / Turiya / Fourth state \-\> what comes *after* working through our thoughts+experiences and on the other end of silence  
    * The unconscious becomes the conduit to the collective consciousness, the *brahman*.   
* We are mostly on autopilot. When we have experiences, we don’t get to “choose” how it’s interpreted or collected in our memories, which is why therapy often deals with misbeliefs about the self.  
* Sleep is critical for the unconscious.  
* Saying “this is my experience, this is true for me and you just don’t understand my life” is self-defeating – you shape and control your experience and the “programming” that you take in, and people who are stuck often are consuming a “trash diet” of occupying and directing their minds towards inputs that create negative/unhelpful framings of our experiences.

### Advanced Quest: Shadow Trait Reflections

*Shadow work involves looking at the traits you dislike in others and how they might reflect in yourself, as well as what purpose that serves. For example, those who pick fights might be doing so in order to have a “real reason” to express anger they are already feeling towards someone. Reflecting on these traits can allow you to uncover what you feel an unconscious need to work on in yourself, and by moving this to a conscious change, you can change this programming towards others.* 

**Activity:**  
Find a quiet space and reflect on at least 3 traits you strongly dislike in others. Think about how these might reflect your *own* suppressed qualities, and what positive purpose these traits might be serving for others. Then, consider what specific actions you could use to achieve these purposes for *yourself* in ways that are more integrated with your conscious mind.

***Example:***   
*Shadow Trait and Self Reflection: I am bothered when others ghost conversations or become inaccessible. This might be because I myself struggle to know how to assert time and energy boundaries in some situations without lashing out, and so I’m bothered by seeing others protect their own time in ways I dislike.*

*Positive Purpose: Ending an interaction or connection with silence enables others to shift away to other activities when they’re low on time or energy without being outwardly aggressive towards others or having to actively say something rude.*

*Specific Action: I can protect myself and my energy without aggression and in a more positive way by letting others know at the beginning of a conversation if my time is limited and inviting them to do the same.* 

**Bonus:** After these reflections, try to notice any shifts in your perceptions of others next time you encounter them displaying these qualities or behaviors. What has or hasn’t changed, and what might that say about your unconscious ideas of the other person and of yourself?

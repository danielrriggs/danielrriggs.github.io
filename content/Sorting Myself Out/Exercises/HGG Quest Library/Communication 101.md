## **Lecture 1: How to Express Yourself Clearly**

### **Outline**

* Discussing the objective of expressing oneself clearly and understanding the general process of speech and its potential errors.  
* Exploring the neuroscience of speech production, including the roles of Broca's and Wernicke's areas, and other relevant brain regions.  
* Outlining the process of speech that leads to fluid expression.  
* Analyzing the source of one’s lack of belief in clarity \+ errors in perception.  
* Techniques for organizing thoughts and practicing speech.  
* Understanding the role of the audience.  
* Understanding the role of emotions in word choice.  
* The relationship between levels of conscious activity and clarity of speech.  
* Practices to enhance conscious attention to speech.

## **Lecture 2: How to Know What to Say / What Not To Say**

### **Outline**

* Understanding the motivation behind trying to have “correct speech”.  
* Highlighting the importance of understanding one's intentions in a conversation.  
* Understanding the right balance and approach to self-expression and listening.  
* How to understand the frame from which the other person is operating.  
* Correct usage of techniques like open-ended questions and reflective listening.  
* Advice on how to use speech effectively.

### **Weekly Quest**

**Reminder:**  
What practically makes it hard for us to “say the right thing” are two major sources: ego and emotions.

* When we are afraid of their reaction, it makes it hard for us to say the right thing.   
* When we are afraid of their opinion of us, it makes it hard for us to say the right thing.

Think about something difficult you’ve wanted to talk about with someone, and answer the following questions:

* Why do you want to have the conversation? What is the motivation?  
* Who is the conversation for? For yourself, or for them?  
* What are the emotions that you feel that make the conversation difficult?  
  * Look out for “I’m afraid that…” or “I’m worried that…” or “What if…” statements.  
* What ego do you have in the equation…  
  * If the conversation goes well, I will feel XYZ about myself.  
  * If the conversation goes poorly, I will feel ABC about myself.   
* What do you want to offer in the conversation?  
* What makes it hard for you to listen? Some options:  
  * Their emotions. (What emotions are they? Name them \- use the emotions wheel if necessary).  
  * Your tendency to solve the problem.  
* What are some questions you can start by asking?  
  * Use open-ended questions. No questions that have close-ended responses.

After doing this, do a rote activity \- do the dishes, go for a walk, etc. Do not distract your mind, and let this exercise sink in.

The next opportunity you get, attempt to have the conversation and try to be aware of your emotions and impulses. Remember the key parts of the exercise: Ego, emotions, reflective listening, and open-ended questions.

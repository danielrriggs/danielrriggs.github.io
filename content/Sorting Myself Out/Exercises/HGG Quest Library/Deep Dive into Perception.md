## **Weekly Quest**

This quest delves deeper into the power of perception and its applications in daily life and personal growth.

**Objective:** To develop advanced perceptual skills and apply them to problem-solving and self-awareness.

### **Lite Quests:**

1. #### **Exploring your Perceptual Abilities**

**Activity:** For one day, pay close attention to your perceptual experiences. List at least 5 different types of perception you experience, including both external and internal senses. Reflect on how each type of perception influences your thoughts, emotions, and actions.

| Perceived Experience | Description | Influence on Thoughts/Behaviors/Actions |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *Hunger* | *Feeling of emptiness in stomach; shaky on feet*  | *Made me distracted and irritable towards co-workers; was able to focus better after taking a break for lunch* |

**Reflection**: 

* How does being aware of these different types of perception change your understanding of your experiences?  
* Was it easier to identify the influence of some perceptions over others? Which are clearer to you? Which might you tend to overlook?

2. #### **Perceptions and Impulses** 

**Activity:** For one day, pay close attention to any impulse or desire that occurs to you (whether that’s to have lunch, to skip studying and play DOTA, or to take your dog to the park). What is the effect of the choice you make to follow or ignore that desire?

| Description of Impulse | Followed or Ignored? | Effects of the Choice |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *Get fast food* | *Followed* | *Short-term relief of hunger, felt more sluggish later on and spent money when I could have eaten leftovers for free* |

|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |

**Reflection**: 

* How much weight do you currently give the potential *effect* of following an impulse as far as whether or not you follow it?  
* As you worked to actively perceive your desires as or before you follow them, what did you feel? Did this kind of perception change the outcome versus what you might have chosen in the past?

3. #### **Perception and Past Problem-Solving** 

**Activity:** Choose a recent decision or judgment you made. Analyze this using the following steps:

1. Describe the situation and your decision.  
2. List the key perceptions that influenced your decision.  
3. Identify any potential biases, limitations, or jumps in logic that could have led to your perception.  
4. Consider how different perceptions might have led to a different decision.

**Reflection**:

* What did this analysis tell you about your decision-making process?  
* What steps can you take to improve your perceptual abilities for better decision-making in the future?

### **Medium Quests**

1. #### **Connecting External vs. Internal Perception**

**Activity:** For one day, practice being aware of both your external environment and your internal state. Make a list of at least 5 external observations and 5 internal observations. See if you can see any connections, cognitive biases, or logical fallacies in your perception. 

| External Environment | Internal Observation | Possible Connection or Fallacies/Biases |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *Saw a Steam sale that’s 24 hours only* | *Compelled to buy game now* | *Influenced by FOMO despite not wanting the game as much when it wasn’t on sale* |
| *Friend was rude to me* | *Feeling rejected, pissed off; instinct was to insult them* | *I don’t know what’s going on in their day and this might not be about me* |
|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |

**Reflection**: 

* How does paying attention to both internal and external perceptions change your experience?  
* Are there certain cognitive biases that come up commonly for you?

2. #### **Using Perception to Identify Triggers** 

**Activity:** Choose a behavior of yours that you’d like to change. This might be an addictive behavior, self-defeating thoughts, ways you act towards others around you, or something else.  This week, whenever you notice this behavior occurring (or beginning to), record what you are feeling. If you notice after it has already happened, reflect on the feeling without judgment and backtrack to uncover its source.

| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *\[For someone quick to anger\]Slammed door after conversation with brother* | *Negative self-talk and frustration with myself; racing heartbeat* | *Being interrupted repeatedly may have made me feel like my ideas don’t matter and getting caught in the heat of the moment made me stop thinking about what I was doing* |
|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |

**Reflection:** 

* What patterns do you notice as far as when you want to engage in this behavior?  
* What cognitive biases and fallacies lead you to your response?  
* How can you use your perceptions to notice triggers in the future and course-correct before the behavior happens?

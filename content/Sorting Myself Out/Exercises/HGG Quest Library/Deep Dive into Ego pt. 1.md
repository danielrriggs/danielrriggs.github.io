### **Weekly Quest**

#### **Vasana**

* List 5 vasanas that you encounter daily.  
* Now list 5 vasanas that you struggle with overcoming. They don’t have to be the hardest ones in your life. Write down some of the easiest ones.  
  * Pick one vasana that feels like the easiest to overcome from the list, and commit to working on it over the next few weeks.  
* Also, list 5 vasanas that aid you day-to-day.  
  * If you cannot think of any, write down 5 that you would like to cultivate. These should be relatively easy ones.  
  * Pick one vasana that feels the easiest to build from the list, and commit to building it over the next few weeks.

#### **Samskara**

* Recall one event that carries a lot of emotion with it.  
  * If you have trouble feeling emotion, and/or are alexithymic, try looking for any events that you want to discard or dismiss the moment you think about it. Try to catch the dismissal when it happens.  
* Recall the emotions that were left as a result of the event. Write them down. You can use the [emotions wheel](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:786/format:webp/1*ieAJuyRI3-iOVOBnJzkEwA.jpeg) for this exercise.  
* Think back to the original emotional event. What conclusions did you draw after this event? What lessons did you learn? Write these down.  
* In your day-to-day life, try to keep an eye out for these emotions. Try to identify situations or events that trigger them.  
* How do you change as a person when this pattern/samskara becomes active? Describe the person that you become and write it down.  
  * What do you do that feels “uncharacteristic” of you?  
  * What do you do that feels congruent with your identity?  
* Re-think your conclusions. Are there other ways to interpret it?  
  * We are not trying to find the objective truth here \- we want to examine the variety of alternative conclusions that can be drawn from the same event.  
  * As you write down these alternative conclusions, notice any resistance that arises in response to considering them. Sit with this feeling for as long as you can and note what happens.

Here is a very simple example of a samskara and how it shapes our thinking:

| Initial Event | Emotions Experienced in the Event | Initial Conclusions | Triggers in Present Day | Who do you become when the samskara activates? | Alternative Conclusions |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| I was scratched by a cat I wanted to pet when I was 5 years old. | Fear, betrayal, confusion, indignant, disappointed, lonely | Cats are dangerous. Cats hate me. Cats will attack me. | Neighbors cat entering my apartment. | I become hostile and feel threatened. I freeze and want the cat to leave, but I also don’t want to hurt it. I want to scare it into leaving. I don’t want to deal with this situation. | The situation was inappropriate and I tried to pet a feral cat. I was too eager to pet the cat and my eagerness was perceived as a threat by the cat. |

#### **Klishta**

Think of the following:

* Something you absolutely love.   
* Something you like.  
* Something you feel neutral about.  
* Something you dislike.  
* Something you hate.

The above can be anything: an activity, an object, a person, a location or environment, etc.

Now, think of each of these in order, and write about:

* The first experience that comes to mind.  
* The experience that has the strongest emotions attached to it.  
* The last time you experienced something related to it.  
* (Bonus) The experience that is fairly neutral and doesn’t come to mind easily.

**Analyze**

* Reflect on the above exercise and notice the coloring that your mind adds to things.  
* What are the objective qualities of the thing?   
* What does your mind add to the thing? How much is it based on experience? What other things is it based on?  
  * “I don’t like dogs”  
  * Dogs are neither good nor bad – they are just dogs.  
  * The rest is added by your mind.   
* Try this with people in your life – notice how your judgment of their actions is “colored” by your experience.

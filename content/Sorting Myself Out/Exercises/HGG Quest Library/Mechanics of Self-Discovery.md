## **Weekly Quest**

Self-discovery involves examining who we are from both a scientific and a spiritual perspective.   
This quest explores how our internal desires, values, and beliefs shape our identity.

**Objective:**  To thoughtfully explore your inner desires, values, and the conditioning that shapes your beliefs and actions.

#### **Lite Quests:** 

#### **Values Identification**

Begin identifying what you value most in your personal and professional life by connecting them to what you already do and where you are spending your time. 

**Activity:** List 10 frequent activities that you spend most of your time doing (e.g., working, reading, socializing, etc.). Next to each activity, note the underlying value(s). Review your list and select the top three that appear the most frequently or seem most important to you and use that in the reflections below. . 

| Activity | Value | Why |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *Weekly HG stream* | *learning, well-being* | *I enjoy learning new things from the streams as it helps me to feel like I’m developing myself the way I want to develop.*  |

**Reflection:** Journal or meditate on these values. Reflect on whether your current lifestyle aligns with these top values. Consider any changes you might want to make to better align your actions with your values.

#### **Sensory Input Reflection**

Start exploring how external influences can cloud judgment and decision-making.

**Activity:** Keep a daily log of all media consumed for a week (e.g., news, social media, TV shows, movies, books, etc.) Reflect daily on its impact on your opinions and emotions. How did these media influence your thoughts and feelings today? Were there moments you felt your opinions swayed by what you consumed? 

**Example**:   
Day: Monday 5/\_/24  
Media: Tech Podcasts, industry news, Instagram, Tik Tok  
Influence: Increased stress about keeping up with tech trends. Worried that my laptop might already be out of date. This made me feel more awkward in public when I was trying to do work at the local coffee shop. 

**Reflection**: Review your log at the end of the week and identify patterns where external media might be overly influencing your beliefs or decisions. Consider the steps you could take to reduce this influence if necessary.

#### **Medium Quest:** 

#### **Values and Emotional Responses**

Explore how your emotional responses can illuminate your underlying values.

**Activity:** For one week, track moments where you experience strong emotions, both positive and negative. At the end of the week, review your journal and note which events triggered the most significant emotional response. For each of the significant events, ask yourself what values were being supported or challenged. 

| Emotion | Trigger | Related value(s) |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *Frustration in the meeting on Tuesday* | *Team meeting was held up by 20 minutes because a colleague was unprepared* | *Respect, it does not appear that this colleague respected our time enough to be prepared for the meeting* |

**Reflection:** Reflect on how well your current environment and your daily choices support your core values based on your emotional responses.

#### **Belief Origin Journaling**

Explore the origins of your core beliefs and how they shape your reality.

**Activity:** Write down five strong beliefs you hold about life, work, relationships, etc. For each belief, use journaling to delve into its origins:

* When did you first start believing this?  
* Who influenced this belief (parents, teachers, friends, media)?  
* Write a detailed account of this initial experience. Focus on factual details, describing the scenario scene by scene.  
* For each belief story, note the emotions you felt at the time and any emotions that arise while recalling it. For each key moment in the story, answer:  
  * What were you feeling at this point?  
  * How did these feelings evolve as the situation progressed?

**Reflection:** Reflect on whether the emotions linked to this belief are still present in your life today. Assess whether these beliefs still serve you well or are based on outdated or external ideas that don't align with your current values or goals.

**Ajna Chakra Meditation**

Enhance mental clarity and focus by concentrating on the Ajna Chakra.

**Activity:** 

* Find a quiet space and sit with your eyes closed.  
* Focus on the area between your eyebrows (the third eye location) and visualize a bright light growing with each breath.  
* Continue for about 15 minutes, bringing your mind back gently whenever it wanders.

**Reflection:** Journal about any insights, thoughts, or feelings that arose during the meditation.

#### **Advanced Quest: ** 

#### **Emotional Response and Mindfulness**

Deepen emotional awareness and resilience by observing emotions without judgment and understanding their transient nature.

**Activity:** Practice a daily mindfulness meditation focusing on emotional sensations. Set aside 20 mins to meditate, breathing deeply and focusing solely on observing any emotions that arise without reacting to them. After each session, log the emotions you observed and any thoughts or judgments that came with them. Note how it felt to let these emotions pass without reacting..

**Reflection**: Review your log at the end of a set period (e.g., one month) to see how your emotional responses have changed over time. Assess how this practice has affected your emotional resilience and your ability to cope with emotional numbness.

#### **Bias Identification and Deconstruction**

Identify and address personal biases that cloud your judgment. 

**Activity:** 

* For one week, keep a journal of all instances where you catch yourself making snap judgments about others or yourself.  
* At the end of the week, analyze these instances to understand the underlying beliefs.  
* Bias Deconstruction \- For each judgment, ask yourself:  
  * Why did I make this judgment?  
  * What beliefs underlie this judgment?  
  * How might these judgments be limiting my understanding of others and myself?  
* Choose one bias to actively work against for another week. For example, if you judged someone's decision as impractical, try to find reasons why it might actually be sensible.

| Date | Situation | Judgement Made | Immediate Thoughts/Response |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *5/12* | *During a coop game online* | *Thought teammate’s idea was impractical* | *It’s too complex and unrealistic* |

After logging the judgments for a week, analyze the judgments to uncover deeper insights and patterns. 

| Judgement Made | Why Did I Make This Judgement | Underlying Beliefs | Limitations Caused by This Judgement |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *Teammate's idea was impractical* | *It was too complicated to work for the situation we were in* | *I believe simple solutions are more practical in high-risk games* | *Dismiss ideas too quickly and you may miss potential innovative solutions* |

**Bahiya and Antar Trataka (Fixed Point Gazing)**

A practice to help stabilize the mind and enhance concentration through focused internal visualization.

**Activity:** 

* Sit in a dark room with a candle placed about three feet in front of you. Gaze at the flame until your eyes water.  
* Close your eyes and see the after-image of the flame in the dark space behind your eyelids (*chidakasha)* for as long as the image remains clear. Try to hold it there. Notice the correlation between your effort and the persistence of the image.  
* Do it 1-2 times in a session.   
* Build a regular practice, increasing the duration of visualization as your concentration improves. Never continue if you experience pain.

**Reflection:** Keep a log of how long you can maintain the visualization over time and note any increase in mental clarity or focus.

(this is not a meditation):

Sit in a comfortable and steady posture. A steady posture is one that puts your head, neck, and trunk in a straight line. If you can't sit comfortably on the floor, you can sit in a straight-backed chair with your head, neck, and trunk erect. This is called the "friendship pose" in the Buddhist system of meditation.

Sit quietly. Breathe diaphragmatically (on exhale: ab muscles will come in & expel the spent gas. ab muscles assist the diaphragm in expanding).
Count from 1 to 100 (in your head). Count from 100 to 1. Note the mental distractions. They tell you about your mental suppressions or procrastinations. Consider the origins of these disturbances. You will learn many things about yourself.
Eventually you can extend this exercise up to 1000. You should do this exercise for at least two minutes every day to retain the power of your memory.

Don't allow your mind to become congested. Your mind is preoccupied with the duties that you have assumed. Learn to keep the mind free for at least a few minutes without interruption, distraction, or interference.

As you learn to do this exercise, you are learning to lead the power of the mind - your thinking process and concentration.

The headstand can be beneficial in developing your memory. Symbolically, standing on the head means learning to stand on your own feet and having complete control and confidence in yourself. Fear of doing the headstand means there is a great fear lurking in your heart. If you can't do a headstand, close your eyes and imagine doing it mentally, first. You cannot do anything effectively if you cannot first do it mentally. Then you'll be able to do things physically.

There are other exercises for memory as well. In the chakra system you may meditate or focus your mind on a particular point. For example, the pineal gland center - between the eyebrows. This is visualized as a small circle.

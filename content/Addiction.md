Alias: Addicted, addict

You want to figure out something in your life that's worth not getting drunk & screwing up, because that's fun.

Prohibition (trying to be 'straight') doesn't work. You need an adventure. Something to do. Something worth waking up for. That's the substitute for the addiction. Actually, the addiction is the substitute for your life adventure.

Why do people pursue rewards that don't produce this resonance? They don't have a value hierarchy. The kids on pleasure island default to hedonistic behavior in the local timeframe because they don't have an [[Identity (philosophy)|Identity]].

Addiction is an adaptation to our environment, not simply a chemical hook. You have to isolate a rat to get it addicted to cocaine. If we aren't on the true adventure of our life, which will be signaled by optimal dopaminergic function (exploratory and playful circuits), then we'll look for false adventures - some of them will be addictive / pathological. 

[Why People Become Addicted | Jordan Peterson](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/6954tvRNz14)

---
There's a chain of [[Behavioral reinforcement|Reinforcement]]. Events around a fix feed & strengthen the addicted personality you've grown.

The physiological effects of addiction can be cured pretty quickly - 1 - 2 weeks. The cognitive components (thoughts & desires) can survive way longer. You may have to rebuild your entire personality. Religious transformation, total personality conversion, is the most effective treatment for [[Alcoholism]].

Solution:
- Build another circuit to shut it down.
	- Stress can disrupt weak circuits.
- Starve it - degrade it over time - by not giving it what it wants - in all the multiple contexts you've associated with the vice.

[Jordan Peterson On Addiction, Detoxing, & Recovering Using Traditional & Alternative Treatments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HtulfoE5mbc)

---
Often times, addiction is self-medicating an **emotion** you're unaware of. We have problems we just can't get a handle on:
*I can't bring myself to work hard; go to the gym; control this behavior; do this or that, etc*. 

**Emotions** are powerful **motivators** and **control behavior**. Just because you can't feel suppressed emotions doesn't mean they aren't wreaking havoc. See [[Emotional Awareness]].

The [[Autonomic Nervous System]] governs our modes of operation. ANS is Divided into:
1) [[Sympathetic Nervous System]]
2) [[Parasympathetic Nervous System]]

The SNS & PNS alternate back & forth throughout the day, depending on what we need. In Eastern systems of medicine, they correlate the SNS & PNS with Sun & Moon, or, active and inactive.

In the case of [[Trauma]], we have an overactivation of the SNS. Our body interprets signals from the outside which trigger SNS responses way more than PNS responses. We need to be able to detect the smallest sign of danger, because if we miss small signs of danger, then we can't protect ourselves. So, we're constantly paying attention to our environment, and it takes a small signal to send us into fight / flight mode. The body is always telling you "you're in danger" even if your [[Mind]] says "this is irrational". It doesn't matter because your body is wired to be in danger.

Examples:
- Abuse
- Neglect - ex: vigilant parents make the environment safe. If parents are neglectful, the world is a dangerous place.
- Highly demanding parents - when we need to perform highly, the SNS can become hyperactive.
	- Pattern: the child has periods of intense activity followed by periods of intense laziness.

Historically, therapists have done everything cognitively - discuss worries, dreams, fears, nightmares, etc. One of the most recent advances in trauma treatment is the realization that all this stuff is not in our heads. A healthy body supports a healthy mind. The best parameter to look at that is [[Heart Rate Variability]]. A traumatized person will have lower HRV. Their heart rate gets wired into a narrower window. Their body's capacity to handle external stressors will be reduced. They've used so much of their reserve capacity that, at baseline, small stressors cause problems. Interventions that increase HRV fix these problems. Athletes like marathon runners have high HRVs. Practices like [[Yoga]], [[Tai Chi]], and [[Meditation]] can increase HRV, relax our physiology, and reduce our cortisol levels.

EEG is a bad tool to measure quality of [[Meditation]]. It turns out that HRV is the best tool that we have to measure the quality of Meditation. HRV increases with deeper states of meditation. Relaxation is an important way to rewire our physiology, but we also wanna stress it with exercise. Intense cardio for short periods can be helpful for cases like [[Trauma]].

When the SNS is engaged very hard (such as in 60 seconds HIIT exercise), it triggers a PNS response.

When the SNS activates, two major hormones release - cortisol & adrenaline. Adrenaline takes less than a second to kick in and lasts for about 5 minutes. Cortisol floats around in our body for about four hours and has effects in our body for about 24 hours after release. 

Cortisol increases our blood glucose level (because muscles need glucose). This is one of the reasons why stress makes a disease like diabetes worse - because cortisol and stress causes our blood to be flooded with sugar. There are some foods like beta-glucans, which are found in mushrooms, as well as things like dark chocolate or some healthy fats that have been shown to affect our glucose metabolism and actually feed back and lower our cortisol levels.

The main thing that we wanna do when our physiology has been wired to a high arousal level is to calm down the physiology. We need to teach our body the lesson that not everything needs to trigger danger. The most effective way that we've sort of learned is to increase our heart rate variability.

The last thing that we can do is directly act on cortisol through the blood glucose mechanism.

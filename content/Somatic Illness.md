The link between our body and our past traumas was figured out over 100 years ago.

Early psychoanalysts like Freud observed patients with vague physical complaints (e.g., headaches, stomach aches, pain) that lacked a medical origin. They found that emotional breakthroughs during psychoanalysis resolved these physical symptoms, leading to the belief that physical problems could originate in the [[Mind]]. This isn't true.

In modern medicine, "psychosomatic illnesses" refer to conditions with **both psychological and somatic (bodily) origins**. However, about 30 years ago, "psychosomatic" often implied a purely psychological origin, leading doctors to dismiss conditions like irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), fibromyalgia, and POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardic syndrome) as "all in your head". These patients were often referred to psychiatrists or therapists because conventional medicines were ineffective, yet therapy seemed to help.

Today, there is a clear understanding of the **tight link between mind and body**, acknowledging that they constantly interact.

###### Panchakosha Theory

[[Panchakosha Theory]] explains humans as being composed of five layers. In clinical trials, some energetic practices (like [[Yoga]] & [[Tai Chi]], which are energetic in nature & work on the [[Pranamaya Kosha|Pranamayakosha]]) outperform pure physical practices on various illnesses like osteoarthritis.

In the East, people separated out emotions as fundamentally different from our analytical capability & assigned them different layers - [[Manomaya Kosha]], [[Vijnanamaya Kosha]]. They said emotions and thoughts are different things entirely and aren't part of one [[Mind]]. They thought that we have two separate minds in the same way that we have multiple organ systems in the body.

Panchakosha Theory explains how **one layer can support another layer** if it's assaulted. If my energy is robust I can fight off a physical infection more easily. If I get an emotional assault (bad news from a phone call), if I'm intellectually strong, can my cognition be used to support and protect my emotions? Can I rationalize / reframe this thing?

The other thing Panchakosha Theory discusses how **damage in one layer reverberates** through all the other layers. If I receive bad news, that shock to my emotional self will redistribute itself to all of the other selves (parts of being). We actually have good scientific evidence for this. If I receive bad news, then that emotional impact will cause changes in my immune functioning. There are studies that show that your emotional state affects the rate at which you respond to vaccines.

The main thing to take away is that **all of these dimensions of being are connected**. 

###### Principles through which trauma affects the body
This is what we see in the somatic manifestations of [[Trauma]].

Research, which started with Freud, has shown that a lot of these vague things that don't have a clear biological origin, like fibromyalgia, IBS, or POTS, are highly associated with [[Trauma]].

We have elucidated some of the mechanisms through which a trauma manifests in the body. Trauma hyperactivates the [[Sympathetic Nervous System|SNS]]. We see more adrenaline release, cortisol release, hyperactivity of the immune system. Cortisol releases white blood cells into the blood. When the immune system is hyperactive and has nothing to fight, this results in whole body inflammation. We see an an overarching inflammation of the body in most of these psychosomatic (trauma related) conditions.
- Fibromyalgia - diffuse whole body pain - associated with high levels of inflammation and stress hormones.
Inflammation also increases sensitivity to pain. We're wired this way because we need to attend to injury if we're bitten / injured / etc.

A traumatized person is in a state of hyperarousal, and is **wired** for hypersensitivity to their environment. Then, it becomes very easy to trigger our stress response, which creates all kinds of physical manifestations.

We know that when people have a history of trauma, especially when the trauma is physical or sexual in nature, people will respond by numbing parts of their body. This is adaptive. We see in people with severe injuries that the body releases endogenous endorphins (pain-numbing chemicals) to down-regulate that pain signal. In physical or sexual [[Trauma]], physical and emotional signals from the body can be shut off. This is in some way mediated by the [[Thalamus]]. We create a situation where we are in some way numb to our body.

This can create a bad cycle. I'm numb to my body, problems can start to build up and I won't detect it. Things get worse over time. Eventually, despite the numbing effect, the signal breaks through to my awareness. And when it breaks through to my awareness, it's very severe.

This creates a very negative experience of the body. Because the only time I'm aware of the body is when the body is in a lot of trouble. This creates a terrible cycle. Every time I'm aware of the body, some thing is wrong, because I couldn't detect it when it was small. This itself can be traumatic in nature, and can increase our sensitivity to the body. We see this paradoxical response in trauma where at the beginning, we numb our body, and then over time we become hyper sensitive (because we don't want to be in that situation again - where we didn't notice a pain signal until it grows to 80% or 90% strength).

This is mediated by a part of our nervous system called our [[Visceral Afferent Nerves]]. Our visceral afferents become very sensitive. Suddenly, benign signals become problems. We've flipped from numb to hyper sensitive. This is the pattern that's present in most of the psychosomatic illnesses.
- *When someone has a panic attack, they can become paranoid about normal fluctuations in their heart rate - misinterpreting them as a panic attack. They start catastrophizing, turning on stress systems, becoming hyper aroused. Because cortisol increases pain sensitivity, everything starts hurting more. Because everything starts hurting, something must be wrong, causing more stress response - a bad cycle.*

When you suppress your emotions & you're mentally numb, that suffering in some way manifests in the body - we aren't sure what the mechanism is. Plenty of studies show that when you suppress your emotional system, your physical symptoms will get worse. Chances are it's related to hyper arousal.

Because I'm numb to my fear or [[Anxiety]] doesn't mean they aren't active. The central theme of what we're discussing is: **just because you don't feel an emotion doesn't mean it isn't wreaking havoc on your system**. Fear & [[Anxiety]] over activates the [[Sympathetic Nervous System|SNS]] which later manifests as physical symptoms.

###### Treatment
We don't have good treatments for most psychosomatic illnesses (because we can't track back to one discrete problem), but interventions like [[Yoga]] or [[Tai Chi]] can calm down the immune system, suppress that hyperarousal.

Healing Somatic Illness:
- **Reconnect With Body**
People who have been traumatized disconnect from their body. We need to reconnect with the body. [[Yoga]] and [[Tai Chi]] are good for this. Exercise where you're really in your body. Connect to your body in a non traumatic way.

- **Physiologic Rewiring**
As we go through the process of physiologic stretch, we re-calibrate our [[Central Nervous System|CNS]] to a more healthy level.

- **Emotional Processing**
Manage your emotions. We can affect physical change through processing emotions. Studies show that [[Psychotherapy]] can improve the symptoms of things like IBS or fibromyalgia. The benefit of psychotherapy is it can get to the core issue of whatever other sub problems exist which manifest as physical problems. The other benefit is that, living with conditions is in itself traumatic. Stress can manifest as an exacerbation of living with the conditions of other traumas. Research is limited, but we are hopeful for more evidence that processing deep-seated traumas can lead to very profound resolution or even complete cures for conditions (like tinnitus) which are generally viewed as lifelong. We have plenty of evidence that managing emotions will improve physical symptoms.

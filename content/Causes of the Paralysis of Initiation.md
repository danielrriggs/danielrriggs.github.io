Lots of people have a hard time getting started. Some people seem to be able to "just do it".

The original paper on Complex [[Post Traumatic Stress Disorder|PTSD]] uses this term "Paralysis of Initiation". In [[Post Traumatic Stress Disorder|PTSD]], you have one or a short term series of [[Trauma|traumatic]] events which develop into a disorder (a psychiatric problem) as a result of the intensity of that singular trauma. In Complex PTSD, the traumatic influence is persistent over time. It's not a one-time thing. It could be an environment you grew up in. Or prisoners of war in a camp for years.

When a trauma is repeated, it messes up the ability to start our own actions. It tampers with our autonomy. These people became listless. When POWs were released from camp, they struggled to initiate things in their own lives.

1) The **first** cause of paralysis of initiation is a **loss of autonomy** and a **lack of reinforcement**.

In a supportive household, when a child initiates something, it's supported. In a controlling environment, what the child wants to do is irrelevant. What the parents want the child to do is what's important. The behavioral impulses aren't reinforced. Instead of autonomy, the child is taught compliance.

The same is true in neglectful households. A child's desires don't get reinforced. The world around me doesn't help me. If I develop autonomy, I get punished for it.

2) The **second** aspect of paralysis of initiation has to do with the **loss of the future dimension**.

If you have controlling parents, planning for your future is a waste of time. In a healthy household, thinking about the future is rewarded. In bad cases, we get punished for thinking about the future. Ex: *I planned something for the weekend, but my parents started drinking and I had to cancel my plans last minute.* Over time, the lesson you learn is that it's better to just have no desires in the first place and out forth little effort. I'm living my life reactively and have no autonomy or control. If I'm stupid enough to want things or exert control, that will just lead to hurt and disappointment. Then, you learn a very dangerous lesson - wanting things and thinking about the future is dangerous and harmful. This lesson leads to the paralysis of initiation later in life.

3) A **reliance on perfectionism** keeps people who grow up in these kinds of environments from initiating things.

This can be tracked back to [[Trauma]].

Attentive parents will protect a child from the dangerous world. Those children become secure in the world around them. The world is a safe place because mom and dad are there to protect me from danger. These children become more confident, take risks, try new things, and explore the world.

A controlling or abusive household creates situations that are very stressful and dangerous for the child. Nobody is protecting me from the world, so it hurts me. Controlling parents will be angry with children for not being perfect. In abusive households, the child learns that there is a magical formula to avoid the abuse. If the child can be kind enough, sweet enough, demure enough, or invisible enough. **Perfectionism and safety go hand in hand**. Perfectionism and safety become one and the same. The world they see does not allow them to make mistakes.

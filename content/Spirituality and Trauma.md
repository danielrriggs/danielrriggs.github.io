Psychiatry has been pretty good at treating [[Mood Disorder]]s, [[Anxiety]] disorders, and [[Depression]], but not [[Trauma]].

Outcomes improve when we inject [[Spirituality]] into evidence-based psychiatry models.

The following have components from eastern spiritual traditions:
- [[Dialectical Behavioral Therapy|DBT]]
- [[Acceptance and Commitment Therapy]]
- [[Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing|EMDR]]

Spiritual change is one the variables that correlates with [[Post Traumatic Growth]].

We don't really know what [[Spirituality]] is. We're at the barrier / frontier of science. We just know that if we inject these traditions into our protocols for treatment, it helps. Clinically, it sems that the more we inject, the more progress we see.

Medical science is founded on the principle that an expert does something to you. It's how all of science's studies are organized. This is a problem when it comes to self-help. [[Spirituality]] is the study of the self from the inside (the subjective experience). We have thousands of studies for what someone else can do for your [[Mental Health]], but far fewer for what you can do for yourself. This is a deficiency in our approach to healing.

The focus of the spiritual tradition is for you to do something.

Evidence-based protocols for the treatment of [[Trauma]] use [[Grounding Meditation Technique|grounding techniques]], not [[Mindfulness]] techniques.

The purpose of [[Meditation]] is to [[Dharana|focus]] the mind on one point. Any time we want to [[Dharana|focus]] the mind on one point, we need an [[Alambana]]. [[Yogis]] figured out that if we have an object to [[Dharana|focus]] on, it's easier to [[Dharana|focus]] the mind.

I can look at a sunset, chant a [[Mantra]], smell something, use prayer beads, etc.

The more powerful the sensory input is, the more our [[Mind]] will be focused.
*Example: Holding an ice cube can snap you out of your emotions, preventing your mind from thinking about tomorrow.*

Cutting minimizes the damage to the self while maximizing the pain. Studies on self-harm behaviors show that the more negative their mind is, the more drawn they are to the self-harm behavior. We also see an increase in cutting when we remove someone's other coping mechanisms. People will organically discover that they can use certain techniques that will focus their mind.

Our first goal in terms of learning to [[Meditation|Meditate]] is learning what our **life jacket** is. What is a technique that I can use that will ground me in the present so I don't get swept away by the flood?

We can start with things like holding an ice cube, which is a very common [[Dialectical Behavioral Therapy|DBT]] technique. If we need a stronger stimulus, we can do [[Ice Diving]].

You can do [[Box Breathing]] or other breathing exercises.

We need a good relationship with our physical form. We can use mind-body practices like [[Yoga]] or [[Tai Chi]] to bring the [[Awareness]] to the present.

Transient states of discomfort are a good thing. If you have a bad relationship with your body (such as in the case of IBS), you aren't in control of the discomfort. When we learn to induce discomfort, then relax, we rewire our relationship with discomfort, which has interesting neuroscience implications. When we engage in practices like [[Yoga]] or [[Tai Chi]], it frees up the part of our brain that governs touch. When we experience pain, our brain zeros in on the pain and we can't focus on anything else. Mind body practices enable us to notice all of these other sensations in our body instead of just the negative ones. It will ground us in our body and will prepare us for the next stages of [[Meditation]].

If talking about [[Trauma]] triggers me, then if I engage in **sensory attentional practices** (like [[Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing|EMDR]]), my attention is distracted from the trauma. As I separate my attention from the recollection of the trauma, I'm almost doing something like [[Exposure Therapy]] for the trauma.

The goal of sensory attentional practices is to focus and control our [[Attention]]. We want it to go where we tell it to. The goal of these practices is to train our [[Attention]].

The problem in [[Trauma]] is we don't control our mind. Our [[Mind]] controls us.

Comentary on the Yoga Sutras of Sage Patanjali
By Swami Satyananda Saraswati

[[Sutra]] means "thread". Implies continuous thought.

The Yoga Sutras are 196 [[Sutra]]s written by Sage Patanjali. The scripture is also called Yoga Darshana, which is widely translated as "Philosophy of Yoga", but Darshana literally means 'to see'. It is derived from drish, meaning 'to see' and is related to the words 'drashta', meaning 'the seer'.
Therefore, Yoga Darshana means 'a process of seeing through yoga', but it does not mean seeing with the eyes or any other outside sense. It means to see something beyond the senses and beyond the mind. A process of seeing with the eyes and other senses closed, and with the mind under complete control. Yoga Darshana is a method of higher perception; it is a means 'to see the invisible' or 'to see with spiritual insight'.




The early Upanishads & the scriptures of Samkhya & Buddhism can't be reliably dated. It's hard to say who influenced who & which text came first.

The Samkhya system dispenses with all theories of God; it says the existence or non-existence of God is irrelevant to a personal [[Sadhana]]. Buddha said the same thing. He taught neither belief nor disbelief in God.

Patanjali differs from the Samkhya - he introduces the concept of God as a means of [[Sadhana]] but only for those inclined towards the path of [[Bhakti Yoga]].

It is interesting to note that Patanjali does not directly quote or refer to any other texts.

The identity & date of Patanjali and the Sutras doesn't matter to us - we're interested in its content. It contains timeless wisdom that is the heritage of all mankind; it is applicable to all ages and all people.

[[Eight Limbs of Raja Yoga]]

The [[Yama]]s & [[Niyama]]s are intended to harmonize one's social interactions & inner feelings to reduce friction between one's outer actions and inner attitudes. There's a two-way relationship: the mind stimulates external actions and external actions stimulate the mind. If the external actions aren't harmonious, then the mind will be disturbed. Conversely, a disturbed mind will tend to produce disharmonious acts. It's a vicious circle. The [[Yama]]s and [[Niyama]]s intend to break this vicious circle and thereby calm the mind by sensible actions and sensible attitudes toward oneself, toward one's life and towards one's surroundings.

[[Asana]] defined by Patanjali is a steady and comfortable sitting position. The purpose of Asana in [[Patanjali Yoga]] is to balance the different nerve impulses, feelings of pain and pleasure, heat and cold and all other sensations.

The most important thing is that our **spine** is **straight**.  Tt doesn't matter whether you're laying down or sitting up or standing up.

Posture is really about physics and center of gravity.

Usually chairs have a slope to them. Most chairs are not very conducive from a center of gravity standpoint to having us sit up properly.

There's one very simple principle which allows us to sit up with a proper posture effortlessly. And that is that our **hips** have to be **higher** than our **knees**.

if I take something like a pillow and I actually put it right underneath my hips (to elevate the hips) but not underneath my knees, it's much easier to sit up straight.

The key thing that we want to be looking for is to pay attention to the relationship between our hips and our knees.

Most meditation is actually conducted on the floor, not in chairs.

Cross-legged on the floor, if you sit on something, it'll elevate your hips above your knees and make it easier. The reason meditators cross their feet (half lotus or full lotus posture) on their thighs is because it lowers their knees, which reduces the muscular effort for you to sit up straight. The yogis developed these postures because they wanted you to channel the weight of your body through this central pole of your spine and directly into the ground.

Postures:
- Cross legged
- Half Lotus
- Full Lotus
- Vajrasana (thunder pose) - is sitting on your knees.
- Another posture is one where you actually sit on top of your ankles.

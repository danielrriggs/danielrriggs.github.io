**Meaning** ruler, governor, commander, and which is commonly used for God.

Supreme Consciousness. Supreme [[Purusha]].

Some religions believe in a personal God; some look upon it as the ruler or creator of the universe; some that there is only universal consciousness. [[Yoga]] philosophy is an offshoot of Samkhya, which is an atheistic doctrine.

There are three conceptions about God.
- Firstly, philosophical thinkers in India have viewed him as not different from man, but they have said that [[Atman]] is [[Paramatman]].
- Secondly, it is believed by some that God is in the heart of man and that one does not have to go out to seek for him.
- Thirdly, the greatest number of people believe in the duality of God and man.

[[Yoga Sutra#Sutra 1 24 Definition of Ishwara]]
Ishwara is a particular [[Purusha]] who remains untouched by:
- the five [[Klesha]]s
- three kinds of action
- three kinds of fruits of action
- the seeds of [[Karma]]s

These [[Klesha]]s are rooted in everyone’s [[Mind]], but God is free of them.
![[Klesha#The Five Kleshas]]

**Actions** or [[Karma]] are of three types: good, bad and mixed. They have good, bad and mixed fruits, such as span of life, enjoyment and kind of birth. God is free from all these aspects.

There are three kinds of [[Karma]] have good, bad and mixed fruits. God is free from these aspects.

God is free from [[Karmashaya]].

According to [[Samkhya]], there are many [[Purusha]]s in the universe and Ishwara is the most superior of them. God is a special kind of [[Purusha]], inasmuch as he is completely free of the aspects which are found to bind the other [[Purusha]]s. The other [[Purusha]]s are also free of [[Prakriti]] by nature, but they come in contact with [[Prakriti]] and due to this contact, they go through various births and rebirths. Ishwara is ever free.

Ishwara is the highest manifested consciousness in man. In it there is the seed of all [[Jnana|knowledge]]. The ultimate point of consciousness of divinity in us is [[Samadhi]].

Ishwara is the supreme [[Purusha]] which presides over the infinite [[Purusha]]s. Purusha should not actually mean spirit as is usually thought, but it should mean a particular manifestation of consciousness. He is not in the realm of manifestation but in the realm of the unmanifested state of things ([[Avyaktam|Avyakta]]).

A methodology to apply to a full range (meaning, the steps can be applied universally) of behaviors to liberate yourself from [[Attachment]]s.

A tool for spiritual awakening. A system for ordering your consciousness. Working the 12 steps properly takes you from a state of unconsciousness (not aware) to a state of consciousness (aware).

Addictions are a way of looking for an awakening / a spiritual connection. You're looking for oneness / God.

When we form an attachment to an object, whether it's a physical object, an object in your mind (such as your relationship to your relationships / work, etc), or a neurological compound, that relationship is not conscious, aware, and functional. Therefore, it has the potential to cause you pain.

Instead of seeing a problem in your life as a deficit or a wound, see it as an invitation / opportunity to improve. For example, as soon as you see yourself as angry / irritated / discontent / afraid, etc, don't see that as "something's wrong with me". See it as a signal of something you need to interpret. The fuel behind it is a yearning. A craving / desire to acquire. You're affording an attachment to external phenomena to ameliorate an inner malady. You're trying to make yourself feel better, and that is a spiritual issue. The function of spirituality is to amend the way that you feel - to address a misalignment between inner and outer lives.

You can replace `alcohol` with whatever vice you have in mind.

1) We admit that we are **powerless** over alcohol — that our lives have become **unmanageable**.
	1) Once you've admitted that there is a problem, it's an invitation for reflection (inventory) & there is an **opportunity** to **change** & improve.
		1)  If you accept the possibility of change & choose to proceed to the next steps, realize some of these things:
			1) You can ask for help
			2) You can observe the patterns that habitually reoccur
			3) You can see if anyone is being harmed as a result
			4) You can stay aware.
			5) Your perspective can change from an infatuation with self-fulfillment to a willingness to be of service to others. The is the objective (end result) of the system.
	2) There's a moment of arrest - "I don't want to proceed living any further this way". An honest acknowledgement.
	3) **Identify** what it is you want to change. There is a requirement for **specificity**.
		1) Once you remove alcohol from your life, you're confronted with myriad of other problems in your life that you'll have to change.

2) Believe that a Power greater than ourselves could restore us to sanity.
	1) Acknowledging that your behavior is insane
	2) Step 2 is about hope - inviting and accepting the possibility for change.
		1) We don't live in a universe without options or choices. Everyday doesn't have to be like this. It's possible to not to this.
	3) This moves us from a state where we're dependent upon `alcohol` to the the terrible abyss of not knowing. All addiction and repeated behavior is an attempt to provide a kind of self evoked regulation to hold us in the horror of a world that doesn't provide us with enough structure.
	4) Embarking on a journey of hope is frightening.
	5) The reason for the `alcohol` is because you can't bear to be you. Because there is no you. The entire thing is made of up bio-chemical drives and memories. The whole point of 12 step recovery is to recover the person you're intended to be. This assumes that there's an innate teleology that you're embarking upon. As surely as a seed becomes a tree, nobody questions that ordinary miracle - encapsulated there within the acorn is the miracle of the mighty oak. Few oak trees in their sapling stage have some overbearing father or negligent mother barking at them that they're not good enough. Step 2 stakes you back to a place where you recognize that you were going somewhere - that there is a better version of you. You aren't worthless; your own belief that you're worthless is as much an illusion as is the belief that you can make yourself happy with a life of endless Ferraris and blowjobs.
	6) This is a return to innocence; it's hard for a wounded person to be vulnerable.
	7) This is not austere or some hand-me-down deity from a culture that doesn't love you; it isn't prescribed to you. It's a personal willingness to accept or inquire into the idea of a way out of the synaptic code you've been inhabiting that isn't working.
	8) The alternative (there is no power greater than myself) is even more insane . The narcissistic whiplash from the thought that you've already inhabited all possibility - that this is the only you that you could be - is even more insane.

3) Make a decision to turn our will and our lives over to the care of God as we understood Him.

4) Made a searching and fearless moral inventory of ourselves.

5) Admitted to God, to ourselves, and to another human being the exact nature of our wrongs.

6) Were entirely ready to have God remove all these defects of character.

7) Humbly asked Him to remove our shortcomings.

8) Made a list of all persons we had harmed, and became willing to make amends to them all.

9) Made direct amends to such people wherever possible, except when to do so would injure them or others.

10) Continued to take personal inventory and when we were wrong promptly admitted it.

11) Sought through prayer and meditation to improve our conscious contact with God as we understood Him, praying only for knowledge of His will for us and the power to carry that out.

12) Having had a spiritual awakening as the result of these Steps, we tried to carry this message to alcoholics, and to practice these principles in all our affairs.

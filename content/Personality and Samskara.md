When you go through a life with [[Trauma]], you get shaped as a person and then you have this sense of who you are. If you've been traumatized, parts of what you believe about yourself may not actually be who you are. Change at that level can be very healing.

###### [[Personality]] vs [[Ego|identity]]
We use these concepts somewhat interchangeably. What's the difference? They're very different - opposite ends of the spectrum.

Identity is the way you see yourself. Your sense of "I". Who I am. The [[Ego|ahamkara]].

Personality is the way that you interact with the external world. Personality is the variable way that an individual will respond to a situation

Examples:
- *When going dancing, some people are self-conscious; others are more loose.*
- *When failing a test, some people react with, "my life is over, all my dreams are shattered"; Others react with, "I'm fucked anyway, what difference is an additional 'F'?"*

Personality has three components:
1) Shapes how we interpret information
2) Shapes our internal reaction to information
3) Dictates how we behave

Example words we use to describe personality: Shy vs Outgoing person at a party. Hardworking. Resilient. Go-Getter. A peacemaker. An introvert. All of these aspects to personality can be boiled down to these three things.

How does personality get formed? Some personality appears to be inherited. We don't know how much of it comes from the genetic level vs inherited mannerisms.
*Ex: A two year old sits down and grunts like Grandma does when grandma shits down.*

We know for sure that some personality is formed from our experiences. This is where [[Trauma]] comes in. We know that history of trauma correlates with some personality traits.

Trauma Can Lead to:
1) Narcissistic personality
2) Dependent personality
3) Perfectionistic personality

It's kind of common sense that our experiences shape who we are; but, if we want to understand how to change the person that we are we have to tunnel down into this experience a bit more.

How our personality gets shaped ([[Core Sequence of Trauma]]): trauma is an adaptation. It's a learning. *I become shy because I realized early on that if I get noticed, I get bullied*.

[[Samskara]]s (balls of dormant undigested emotion) live in us and arise when activated under various circumstances. In the East, personality is viewed as the collection of [[Samskara]]s that we have. The experiences we go through in life get stored in us in some kind of memory (emotional energy impression). The way I feel when I see something is a personality trait - a collection of [[Samskara]]s.

If I get bit by a dog, I can develop a [[Phobia]] of dogs. As a psychotherapist, the process of helping with a phobia is very similar to the [[Yoga|Yogic]] process of digesting a [[Samskara]]. Treatment for [[Phobia]]s is [[Exposure Therapy]]. We exposing someone to the stimulus and they have a physiologic response. We're giving them more emotional experiences, but there's no damage. Over time, we can extinguish the physiologic and emotional phobia.

Yogis observed that when we get a trigger, emotions arise. Through the process of observing or even analyzing our emotions, or by doing pranayama practices or meditation practices that allow us to remain tranquil, we can extinguish this samskaric energy.

When Freud would do psychotherapy with his patients, he noticed one of two patterns. Some people would discuss a trauma in an emotionally numb way. Those people didn't get better. The people who recited their story and felt the emotions in the present got better. 

We want to bring up the ball of stored emotional energy (vent it) so we can start the process of metabolizing it. We'll learn how to survive it in the present. Then it will be resolved.

Prepare yourself for the emotional reaction. And as you have this emotional reaction, you can start to manage it in a different way. **Don't** go straight to the behavior that will calm you down (*ex: start scrolling on my phone, which will numb out my emotional circuitry and I'll no longer feel anxious*). That propagates the samskara. It comes up, it wrecks our life for a little bit, and then we numb it out with a cell phone, and then it's just waiting there for us again. Then, we form an even stronger sense of identity.

Instead, use emotional regulation techniques to tolerate the emotion. Breathe through it. Notice what it's doing in your body. Then, it will eventually calm down. Then, push yourself to engage in a slight behavioral change. This is the process of samskaric digestion and personality change.

We can also achieve this process through something like psychotherapy. [[Cognitive Behavioral Therapy|CBT]] is good at this. Psychotherapists are trained to help you gain awareness of this process. When you see something, what is the emotion that you feel? What are the thoughts that you have? What are the behaviors that you have? We can start to really understand and tease apart these things so that we can start to change.

**Summary:**
Personality change / samskaric digestion process: Sensory input triggers things. Emotional reaction comes up. Use emotional regulation techniques to tolerate it. Push yourself to slightly change.

If you're traumatized, you didn't get to pick who you became. Chances are, if you're doing this, there's a part of yourself you don't like. Understanding the process of personality change helps us access tools to become the person we want to be.

Personality is 3 things: How you **see** the world, how you **react internally** to what you see, and how you **behave**. As you change these three aspects to become the person you want to be.

You can change your behavior. You can even change the way you perceive the world. [[Pratyahara]] is about purifying our ability to perceive the world.

There's no such thing as good emotions or bad emotions. If you run from your emotions, your life can become worse.

The body inflicts pain to get you to act. Hunger or thirst are some of the most painful. Emotions are no different. Emotions serve 2 purposes: they're a source of **information**, and they're a **motivator** towards action. Numbing a negative emotion is bad because it's important for you to take an action in a particular direction.

- **Anger** helps us protect our territory. Sometime we feel anger and we don't know why. The brain processes lots of stuff subconsciously. Chances are, some part of you is being violated. Anger increases heart rate & reduces peripheral vision to a 30 degree arc. It's why, during the experience of anger, you can't let something go. Our whole organism zooms in on something we perceive as a threat.

- **Fear** or [[Anxiety]] are related because they both activate the [[Amygdala]]. Fear or anxiety are projected threats. We're worried about things that haven't happened yet. It's our brain's way of warning us of potential danger so that we can act to avoid them. It motivates us to change our circumstances or behavior.

- **Shame** feels bad. It's our brain's way of telling us we're doing something wrong. Shame and guilt are two sides of the same coin. Shame is externally facing. We feel ashamed when someone else is watching us. We're tribal. The respect of the community is crucial for survival. When I feel shame, someone else things I did something wrong.

- **Guilt** is more internally facing. When you've learned lessons about how you should be living life and you deviate from those lessons, you feel guilt. When I feel guilt, I think I did something wrong.

The corrective action for guilt and shame is the same - the internal driver is to change our behavior to satisfy these two things.

- **Sadness** is motivating ourselves towards inaction. This is an important motivation. Sometimes our brain realizes that action is not a good idea, or is insufficient. If there's a problem you consider to be unsolvable, it would be a waste of energy to try to fix it. So instead, the brain induces a mode of action which keeps us inactive so we don't waste resources.

I cry when I feel like there's a problem I can't solve. It triggers in other people a desire to help us.

Chances are, crying or sadness is your body trying to signal to you that trying to solve this problem isn't worth the effort, or that you won't be able to solve this problem on your own and you need help.

Sometimes our interpretation of the environment aren't accurate. Sometimes our emotions can be a source of other people being able to manipulate us.

Depending on how we're wired, we can be **conditioned** to believe that certain things are right or wrong. Shame is a good example. If our parents induced shame to correct our behavior, we'll carry those triggers for a shame response when we aren't perfect. At this point, the suffering isn't adaptive anymore - we don't need to be feeling this way. This is true of all other emotions.

We can be conditioned to feel anger - certain territory isn't actually ours - I feel entitled to a promotion - I deserve it - no one else should get it - I should get it. Or, entitlement to another human being - this is my wife / girlfriend. She is mine. This is the way we were taught to feel these emotions.

Some people will use emotion to **manipulate** us. If someone asks you for money and you say no, they might tell you a sob story to make you feel guilty. To make the guilt go away, you might give them money. This is common in [[Trauma]]. We don't have **emotional regulation skills**. Because of things like [[Hemispheric Lateralization]], we don't even feel these emotions. Since we can't manage our emotions internally, we must rely on actions to fix them.

People get court ordered to take anger management courses - which teach skills to calm down the emotion. If I can calm down the emotion, I don't need to act to fix it.

Technology numbs our emotions. We use technology to suppress our emotions and not take corrective action. With [[Trauma]], we see people lean into external sources for the management of their emotions and they enter a vicious cycle. When people stop solving their problems, they stop moving forward in life and lose momentum.

To break yourself free from the cycle of being manipulated by others, controlled by your emotions, or to break yourself free from an [[Addiction]], we need to learn to listen to our emotions without numbing them. The next thing we need to do is regulate our emotions without giving in to particular behaviors - whether that's playing a video game or loaning your friend $100. Then, that emotional energy will fuel your motivation to take corrective action. When you start channeling your suppressed emotions towards progress, your life will begin to transform.

Sex has all kinds of attachments to it. In a relationship, it serves a few important functions. It's a big part of many relationships. Each [[Attachment Theory|attachment style]] has a unique intersection when it comes to sex.

For many people, sex is an emotional regulation technique. If we look at studies on pornography and masturbation, that anytime we are physically aroused, it suppresses negative emotional circuitry in the brain. People will get [[Addiction|Addicted]] to sex or pornography because when we activate these circuits, these circuits will shut off a lot of negativity in the brain. The process of sex and especially orgasm and what happens after that can release a lot of positive chemicals and neurotransmitters - [[Dopamine]], oxytocin.

[[Trauma]] affects our relationship with sex. Depending on your [[Attachment Theory|attachment style]], sex may mean something different to you than your partner.

In **secure attachment**, we are capable of forming bonds with other people. There are good people out there and there are bad people out there, but the world is fundamentally a safe place. There are plenty of fish in the sea. If one particular relationship doesn't work out, it can still hurt a lot and take time to get past it, but hopefully it's not [[Trauma|traumatizing]] and doesn't change the way I view the world. But, I know that I'm able to form other relationships. 

People with a secure attachment oftentimes will have a string of relationships until they potentially find the one. They're capable of forming bonds, understanding their internal emotional state, and managing some of their own emotions. They're also capable of sitting with their partner's emotions. they can handle some degree of emotionality in their partners without it reflecting on them. They have a sense of [[Ego|identity]] that is pretty solid. They're pretty confident. They have some degree of [[Self-Image|Self-Esteem]]. In the case of secure attachment, sex is an expression of intimacy. It's one of the ways our bond manifests.

In **anxious attachment**, we're afraid of the outside world. We can't count on the world to be a safe place. Sometimes we can count on people to be there for us. Sometimes the caregiver gives me what I need, sometimes they don't. The relationship becomes a game of getting what I need from this person. How do I evoke the right response from this person so that I can feel secure? People who are anxiously attached will use other people as forms of emotional regulation. This person has learned that if I feel bad inside, someone else can fix it.

In relationships, we'll see a lot of clingy behavior. We'll see behavior designed to evoke another kind of behavior. I can be excessively bubbly or positive because I figured out that if I can be likable, people will be there for me & include me. We see a lot of behaviors that evoke a certain sympathy or response from others.

They're concerned about other people leaving them. If this person leaves, I'm left in this dangerous world that I can't handle on my own. This can be frustrating for the person they're in a relationship with. Even though you've demonstrated time and time again that you're going nowhere, this fear never seems to disappear on their end. If you say that you need space from your partner who is an anxiously attached individual, this can send them into a tailspin. They have difficulty understanding that you have your own emotional needs. They're so caught up in their own internal space that they have difficulty attending to the needs of others.

There is a constant fear of abandonment. This can result in perceived threats of abandonment where ambiguous signals from their partner are interpreted in the worst way. If my partner isn't calling / texting me back, this means they're going to leave. In the worst cases of anxious attachment, this grows into [[Borderline Personality Disorder]] at which point the fear of abandonment is so profound that it drastically impairs our function.

 In the case of anxious attachment, we sort of utilize sex as a way to evoke intimacy. We've figured out that sex is a way to bind someone to me.

In **avoidantly attachment**, people are fiercely independent. I've learned through my upbringing that I cannot count on other people to be with me which means I have to handle my own emotional needs by myself. In relationship, these people can come across as cold, distant, at arm's length. There's a lack of emotional connectedness.

Even though avoidantly attached individuals have learned to be fiercely independent, the fundamental need for emotional and human connection never really goes away. fMRI studies of people who are avoidantly attached show that the part of their brain that is hungry for emotional connection is actively being suppressed. They're expending a lot of energy to suppress their natural inclination to connect to another human. They learned early on that, if I try to connect to someone, all I get is pain and disappointment.

They exist in this limbo zone where they crave attachment, but if you get too close, they'll push you away. This can be confusing for both the avoidantly attached individual and the partner because it feels like if we get too close, they're pushing you away, but if they get too far, you're pulling them in.

Avoidantly attached individuals strive for fierce independence. This can create exaggerations of certain strengths. Since I have to do everything by myself, I have to be good at everything. They will have a false perception of their capability. They will downplay the capability of their partner. They will make their partners feel inferior - because if I'm superior and you're inferior, I don't need you, because if I need you, then I'm opening myself up to danger and vulnerability. They tend to be quite cold and detached in relationships. They'll put up a lot of walls. They get uncomfortable if you try to enter into the walls.

Sex can be something to keep you at arms bay from someone else. It's actually used to create separation in the relationship. After sex, there may be some very little intimacy. It's a mechanical and physical act. We may see this in situations like friends with benefits.

Sex is a whole brain and whole body experience. The whole brain activates during sex: the sensory parts of the brain, our emotions, our [[Frontal lobes|Frontal lobe]], the [[Nucleus accumbens]] (pleasure), our sense of identity & connection to other people, the [[Sympathetic Nervous System|SNS]] and the [[Parasympathetic Nervous System|PNS]], a bunch of neurotransmitters - [[Dopamine]], oxytocin, [[Serotonin]] afterwards - all are active. This is a real problem in [[Trauma]].

We've tried to make the case that [[Trauma]] is not an isolated experience. It affects every dimension of your being. If you have a history of [[Trauma]], sex can be difficult to engage in.

If we're disconnected from our emotions, it can be difficult to engage with someone and make love. Sex is a very powerful physiological intervention. It makes all the thoughts leave our [[Mind]]. It focuses our attention on the present. It triggers the [[Parasympathetic Nervous System|PNS]] and the [[Sympathetic Nervous System|SNS]]. Sex can be used as a tool to engage many of the things we have previously discussed for healing [[Trauma]].

[[Trauma]] and sex have a very complicated and intertwined relationship. The good news is that even if you struggle in some way - even if you use sex as a coping mechanism, you have sex way more than you're supposed to, or you have difficulty engaging in intimacy, since it's all [[Trauma]] related, we have many avenues to address these things.

If you have difficulty forming an emotional connection, you have tools to get in touch with and metabolize your emotions - [[Emotional Awareness]], [[Identity (trauma)]], [[Samskara]].

If you have difficulty understanding your relationships with other people, hopefully, in this lecture, we've illustrated some of the dynamics that are at play.

If you're an avoidantly attached individual and you crave connection with another human being, but you're scared of it, so you send people mixed signals (because on the one hand, you don't want them to leave, but on the other hand, you don't want them to get any closer - which is confusing as hell for the other person).

No matter what your problem is, if you spend enough time and energy, and get the right kind of help, chances are you can move forward in a very positive way and you can get exactly to where you want to go, which is hopefully a healthy relationship that involves the amount of sex that you feel comfortable with.

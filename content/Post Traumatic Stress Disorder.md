First discovered in WW1 vets; Shell shock - soldiers weren't directly injured. Explosions near them would concuss them to the point that they had physical manifestations - ringing in the ears (tinnitus), difficulty concentrating / brain fog, aches, pains, stomach problems. The constellation of symptoms couldn't be tracked to a single injury.

We discovered that a [[Trauma|traumatic]] event shapes someone and lingers as a persistent medical problem.

We used to think that PTSD was triggered only if something really bad happened to you, but more research has shown us that all kinds of things can cause it, not necessarily a single event.

A single event that happens to you can trigger PTSD.

More chronic events that aren't immediately traumatic can build up over time and cause PTSD. People like police officers who work homicide cases are constantly exposed to traumatic things and can develop PTSD.

The event doesn't even have to happen to you, or you don't have to witness it directly. We've seen cases in parents who have children who get traumatized, and when the parents discover this, they can manifest symptoms of PTSD.

The threat of an event is sufficient to trigger PTSD. Even if nothing happened, but there was a possibility of something bad happening and you felt threatened, that can trigger this trauma response.

**Impairment of function** is the difference between low-grade trauma and something that qualifies as a psychiatric diagnosis that requires treatment. The difference between being sad after a breakup and being clinically [[Depression|depressed]] after a breakup is the impairment of function that persists over time. People with PTSD have difficulty living their normal lives. They have difficulty engaging in relationships, holding down jobs, being students. The impact of trauma is so great that we cannot live a normal life. This impairment of function must persist for at least one month to qualify for PTSD.

Key symptoms we see in PTSD:
- 1) **Intrusive memories or experiences** that are completely involuntary and out of your control. 
	- It isn't just that the experience happened. It's relived over and over. This can manifest in ways like nightmares, flashbacks.
	- People with PTSD can even be triggered by symbolic connections.

- 2) **Avoidance** - When we have these intrusive, impairing experiences that we can't control, we start **avoiding certain stimuli**. 
- We live lives of avoidance (of triggers), which restricts and shapes our life.
	- For example, if religious iconography is triggering for me, I have to avoid all religious associated things. 
	- We can even avoid things like sleep so nightmares don't come back.

There are two major buckets of adaptations in people with PTSD:
- 1) A change in their capacity for cognition
	- The way our [[Mind]] works fundamentally changes. We can experience things like amnesia, have distorted senses of [[Nature of Self|self]] (have a very negative view of themselves). Frequently, victims of [[Trauma]] will blame themselves for the [[Trauma]] and will feel somewhat unworthy or unclean.
	- People with PTSD will also exist in **persistent negative mood states** - [[Anxiety]], fear, [[Depression]], but they aren't caused by an [[Anxiety]] disorder or a [[Mood Disorder]]. You can still be incredibly [[Depression|depressed]], but the source of that is the [[Trauma]]. Since the [[Trauma]] is persistent within you and you can't shake it, these persistent negative mood states can exist for a very long time.
	- Dissociation or detachment - It is very common for people with a history of PTSD to feel detached from their existence. They have difficulty engaging in and enjoying life - the [[Mind]] doesn't function normally.
- 2) Physiologic changes
	- Can have enhanced startle responses. They have a [[Sympathetic Nervous System|SNS]] that's wired in a high arousal state. They can experience pain, cortisol, activation of the [[Sympathetic Nervous System|SNS]], adrenaline system, the [[Stress Physiology|Stress]] response system. This can make them sensitive to everything from pain signals to being hyper sensitive to sounds in the environment.

PTSD has 4 major buckets of symptoms:
1) **Intrusion** of unwanted **thoughts / emotions** - flashbacks, nightmares. Can be triggered by environmental stimuli or symbolic stimuli. This shapes the way we life life.
2) We live a life of **avoidance** because we can't control this stuff when it happens. When it happens, I lose all control, so I avoid it entirely. We see isolation, avoidance of relationships.
3) We **rewire our brain**, our **cognitive experience**, our sense of [[Self-Image|Self-Esteem]], can experience persistent negative mood states. Dissociation and amnesia.
4) **Rewiring of our physiology** - hyper activation of our [[Sympathetic Nervous System|SNS]].

When we experience this for at least 1 month and it's persistent, that's when we qualify for PTSD.

###### Complex PTSD
Judith Herman wrote a paper on complex PTSD. She observed that some people who have chronic exposure to [[Trauma]] trigger a cascade of changes.

Chronic exposure to [[Trauma|traumatic]] experiences during the time of a human is being developed can cause complex PTSD, which has a slightly different picture than PTSD. It shapes people in a different way. They don't quite have intrusive memories of an isolated memories, because it's normal for them.

Paralysis of initiation is actually rooted in complex PTSD. See [[Causes of the Paralysis of Initiation]], [[Overcoming the Paralysis of Initiation]]. Something about the way that we develop changes the way that we see the world fundamentally and shapes the way that we interact with it.

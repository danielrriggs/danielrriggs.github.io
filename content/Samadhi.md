###### Definition 1
Translated as 'a temporary state of enlightenment'.

A sub-state of [[Dhyana]]. A state when the [[Mind]] is completely gone & you're operating completely at the level of [[Consciousness]].

With prolonged periods of Samadhi, people get transformed & the way they view the world changes.

Spiritual absorption.

The eighth rung of [[Raja Yoga]].

The tranquil state of mind in which fluctuations of the mind no longer arise.

The [[Yoga Sutra]]s are a manual for teachers to get people ready (they aren't for everyone).

A definition from [[Katha Upanishad]] (111:10):

[[Bhagavad Gita]] (2.53):
> When the intellect, having been perplexed by hearing the words of the scruptures, stands immovable in samadhi, then you shall attain self-realization.

From [[Yoga Chudamani Upanishad]]
> Twelve (protracted rounds) of [[Pranayama]] lead to [[Pratyahara]]. Twelve (extended durations of) [[Pratyahara]] lead to [[Dharana]]. Twelve such [[Dharana]]s lead to [[Dhyana]] and twelve such [[Dhyana]]s result in samadhi.

In the state of Samadhi, the aspirant arrives at the pointless point of [[Consciousness]] beyond which no [[Consciousness]] remains; even the sense of individuality does not function.

A state of higher awareness where the [[Sukshma Sarira|Mental Body]] does not function. The [[Atman|Atmic]] vehicle alone functions in that state.

Samadhi begins after your [[Consciousness]] becomes free from [[Maya]]. When [[Consciousness]] is withdrawn from the physical & pranic sense of awareness, but remains mentally aware. When [[Consciousness]] goes deep into the [[Manomaya Kosha]].

[[Atman]] is not a [[Kosha|Sphere of Mind]] (like the [[Manomaya Kosha]], [[Vijnanamaya Kosha]], & [[Anandamaya Kosha]]); it is subtler & finer; it is pure [[Awareness]].

Beyond the five [[Kosha|Spheres of Consciousness]] ([[Panchakosha Theory#The Five Koshas]]) there is ultimate awareness, known as [[Purusha]]. Samadhi is achieved when the consciousness transcends the spheres of object, motion, thought, & instinct and ultimately reaches the sphere of awareness. All the five spheres of [[Consciousness]] are entangled with [[Prakriti]], but when the [[Consciousness]] reaches the [[Anandamaya Kosha]], it becomes practically free from the clutches of [[Prakriti]].

###### The two categories of samadhi
There are two categories of Samadhi, a higher state and a lower state. The lower state is [[Sabeeja]]. The higher (finer) state is [[Nirbeeja]].

###### The four kinds of samadhi
There are four different **kinds** of samadhi for the four different kinds of [[Pratyaya]]s:
- [[Vitarka]]
- [[Vichara]]
- [[Ananda]]
- [[Asmita]]

###### The stages of samadhi progression
Every depth of samprajnata samadhi is intermixed with a state of asamprajnata samadhi
- [[Dharana]] > [[Dhyana]] > [[Vitarka]] [[Samprajnata]] > [[Asamprajnata]] > [[Vichara]] [[Samprajnata]] > [[Asamprajnata]] > [[Ananda]] [[Samprajnata]] > [[Asamprajnata]] > [[Asmita]] [[Samprajnata]] > samadhi.
Asmita Samprajnata samadhi finally culminates in nirbeeja samadhi.

Each state is preceded by practice of the prior state. In vitarka samadhi, the aspirant is aware of an object only, without there being an awareness of anything else. When this practice ([[Abhyasa]]) continues, the consciousness of this [[Pratyaya]] also ceases and that is the state of [[Laya]]. From this state of [[Laya]], the aspirant is either thrown up into the next higher state, or he may again revert to a state of awareness of the [[Pratyaya]]. It may even happen that he reverts to the state of [[Dhyana]] or [[Dharana]].

###### [[Sabeeja]] Samadhi
In sabeeja samadhi, the [[Mind]] is fixed on a [[Pratyaya]] (a symbol), either [[gross]] or [[subtle]].
A beginner has to use a certain **basis** for **fixing the mind**. Through a process of refinement of consciousness, the pratyaya is ultimately thrown away.

[[Samprajnata]] and [[Asamprajnata]] are varieties of [[Samadhi#Sabeeja Samadhi]].

**Samprajnata**
In [[Samprajnata]] samadhi, there is awareness of a [[Pratyaya]].

**Asamprajnata**
In [[Asamprajnata]] samadhi, there is no awareness of any [[Pratyaya]]. This is called [[Virama]] [[Pratyaya]], but the mere absence of awareness of a symbol does not mean [[Asamprajnata]]. This state of [[Laya]] is not free from [[Samskara]]s.
In [[Asamprajnata]] there are two distinct characteristics: 
- Dropping of the [[Pratyaya]]
- The presence of [[Samskara]].

###### [[Nirbeeja]] Samadhi
The higher form of Samadhi.

In [[Nirbeeja]] samadhi, the [[Samskara]] is completely finished & the consciousness is completely dissolved because there is no need of consciousness.

Nirbeeja samadhi is not a state at all; it is a state devoid ofawareness, devoid ofconsciousness. According to [[Yoga]], [[Consciousness]] or awareness is in the form of motion or vibration, but nirbeeja samadhi is not a state of motion or vibration. It involves stillness.

###### Three streams of awareness
In each category of [[Samprajnata]] samadhi, we are fully aware of three things:
- The [[Pratyaya]]
- The process of meditation
- Ourselves as meditators

In the beginning one has a [[Japa]] mantra or a [[Roopa]] as the [[Pratyaya]] for [[Meditation]], but later on that is dropped.

###### Commentary on samadhi progression
Consciousness of the object does not vanish completely; it only changes layers of our past experiences ([[Samskara]]). We usually experience the [[Pratyaya]] through these layers, but when they are dissolved through practice, the [[Pratyaya]] can be clearly seen.

In [[Dharana]] the object appears as the formulated pattern of [[gross]] [[Consciousness]]; in [[dhyana]] there is the concentrated pattern of [[Consciousness]]; in [[Vitarka]] samadhi there is a supramental pattern of [[Consciousness]]; in [[Vichara]] it is a contemplative pattern; in [[Ananda]] it is the bliss pattern, etc., but [[Pratyaya]] remains until the end. It only drops intermittently, which is called [[Virama]] [[Pratyaya]]. In [[Asamprajnata]] samadhi, the [[Pratyaya]] drops, but only temporarily. It again revives itself, but not in the same state of awareness. The state of awareness now may be either deeper or grosser. [[Asamprajnata]] samadhi is only an intermediate state in which the [[Consciousness]] is trying to transcend into a different plane.

Most of us do experience the [[Vitarka]] and other stages as well as the [[Asamprajnata]] stage, but these stages are not stabilized; they are fluctuating. So we should never give up the [[Pratyaya]], whatever it may be - a cross, or a shivalinga, or any object for that matter. We should, through continued practice ([[Abhyasa]]), try to go to deeper states until at last the [[Asamprajnata]] state is reached, but that is indeed extremely difficult.

Where did these theories come from? [[Rishis]] would enter transcendental states in [[Meditation]] and would start to understand things about the universe.

Pay attention to an experience where you had an intuition / revelation / flash of insight about something. [[Rishis]] would take that experience and practice how to get more intense versions of that experience. They engaged in certain kinds of meditative techniques where the principles of the universe around us became apparently clear to them. Then, they started teaching them, and that's where the theory of [[Karma]] comes from.

The theory of [[Karma]] is not something that everyone agrees on. Even within sort of these Eastern karmic traditions like the Hindu tradition and the Buddhist tradition, there is some degree of disagreement about how things actually work.

 In the Hindu version of karma, we believe that you have a [[Atman|Soul]] and that the soul has a karmic debt. It's like a ledger tracking the debts and credits - what you're owed and what you owe to other people - at the level of the [[Atman|Soul]]. After this life, your soul travels somewhere else (even into another body) where it carries the debts with it from the past life into this current life. It's almost as if there's a you-ness that connects a lot of different lives and you have the same [[Karma|karmic]] bank account.
 
In the Buddhist view of karma, that sense of you-ness isn't really there. There are some theories of [[Buddhism]] where they think that [[Karma]] is not really tied to a particular person. In Buddhism, there's just simple cause and effect that gets distributed throughout the world.

When I die, my physical body goes and becomes lots of other things. My mental energy will disperse and then splits apart into lots of other things.

The words that I speak are composed of a thought followed by an action and then an effect that is distributed throughout my environment. If I say something that is malicious, I will create a [[Karma|karmic]] consequence of [[Dukkha|suffering]] to someone who is listening to it.

In some versions of the Buddhist view, there isn't this justice angle to it. It's simply that all causes have effects. And the sense of "I" is actually an illusion. You are just a body, a series of atoms, a series of mental energies, a series of conscious energies, and these things will get dispersed and spread apart. Depending on what each of these energies are like, it'll result in different things down the road.

There are differences in what we think, but there are some things that are generally speaking commonly accepted.

**Theory on Common Misconceptions of Karma:**

1) Karma itself doesn't concern morality. Morality is assigned by humans.
There is no morality in [[Karma]] like there's no morality in Newton's third law of every action has an equal and opposite reaction. The morality is based on human preference. When we call something a good karma, the reason we call it good is because we want the result that this action brings. We prefer certain effects over other effects. Bad karmas are just things that we don't want. Studying is a good karma because we want to get good grades.

2) Karma does not negate the idea of free will.
Philosophers and neuroscientists will debate about the nature of free will versus destiny. Are we fated for some things to happen in this life or do we have some sense of control? Even some neuroscientists will say that our perception of free will is simply a series of chemicals that are interacting in our brain that we have no control over.

But for the theory of karma, (which many people think means that we are destined in some way), this is a very simple debate to resolve - free will creates destiny. If I choose to jump off a cliff, I am destined to fall. My actinons will inevitably have some kinds of consequences.

Now you can even go back a little bit further and kind of say, well, what about free will? Is free will really free will? Is your decision to jump off the cliff really your decision? And this is where the theory of karma says, basically, yeah. Remember, we developed our theory of [[Karma]] from [[Yoga|Yogic]] practice. [[Yogis]] noticed that we have [[Consciousness]], and [[Consciousness]] isn't the same thing as [[Mind]]. We tend to think that [[Willpower|Will]] comes from the [[Mind]].

My [[Mind]] can have a [[Desire]]. And yet, I can [[Willpower|Will]] myself to control that [[Desire]]. Strength of [[Willpower|Will]] has a neuroscientific correlate in the [[Frontal lobes|Frontal lobe]]s.

You have some choice over creating what happens in your life.

3) Karma does not get "canceled out".
A good action doesn't cancel out a bad action in some cosmic ledger. Each action has its own consequence. If I have a plant and choose not to water it for a week, then give it a week's worth of water in 1 day, this is not the same as watering the plant every day.

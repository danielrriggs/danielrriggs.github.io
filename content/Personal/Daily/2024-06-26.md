This isn't intended to brag, but I see nothing but skeptical comments on videos that have anything to do with "mental powers". I have experience & evidence to the contrary.

- "Pain" / "discomfort" could be more specific. The who / what / why / and how one is doing something matter. For example, your body knows that some cold exposure can be beneficial, whereas some burning of the skin is not.
- Instead of recommending that people eat hot dogs and avoid difficult things, if you really want to talk science instead of actually do something, I would have recommended looking into the already established science on Pain Control Gate Theory because you can actually apply this knowledge. This will work even if you're both skeptical and inexperienced because it's 100% mechanical.
	- (Simple example: do 20+ reps of isolateral bicep curl on your left arm to failure. It should be pretty painful. If you sit there, your left arm will hurt. Start the exercise on your right arm. Notice that the pain in your left arm instantly vanishes once you start working the right).
- Intermediate athletes can turn on the body's natural painkillers and advanced athletes can engage in mental processes to turn pain into pleasure (and much more is possible in addition to this).

I do this almost every day in the gym parking lot. Someone at the gym asked me about it the day before, so I told them I would document it for them, once. With practice, the process can be initiated almost instantaneously with a single breath. These screenshots are of me sustaining a heart rate of ~90bpm on Dec 19 for 1 hour while sitting in my car (not contracting any muscles).


I have no cold exposure training (besides mildly cold showers in the winter which don't get that cold in the south) & I've only had the opportunity to try this once (when visiting someone's house w/ a cold plunge), but I have a video of doing 10 minutes in sub 40 degree water and then getting out & using nadi shuddhi breathing to stop the overwhelming whole body shivers. As an experiment, I would switch back and forth between without concentration & then back to nadi shuddhi breathing. When breathing without concentration, the whole body shivers would instantly come back. That's unexplored territory, but something's there.

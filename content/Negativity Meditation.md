We think about negativity as coming from the outside. *This person has a negative energy, my boss is very stressful.*

 In this practice, we want to see: where does the negativity within me come from? We don't want to ask the question, why am I unhappy? The mind will jump to a logical reason why you are unhappy, because we've been so conditioned by this psychotherapy, psychoanalytic perspective of the origin of our unhappiness.
 *There's a lot of scientific evidence that shows that psychotherapy can be very helpful. But this is a different way to approach it.*
The challenge of the psychotherapeutic tradition is that it requires another person to act upon you. The advantage of the meditative tradition is that it is designed for you to do with yourself.

This exercise in some ways is similar to the [[Mentality Materiality Meditation]] exercise, because it's a way of looking within ourselves. But it comes from a different tradition. This exercise comes from a Hindu text, the [[Vigyan Bhairav Tantra]].

Shiva says, anytime you look within yourself, any time there is negativity, there is something underneath. The mind naturally goes to all kinds of logical thoughts as to why you are unhappy.
*I am unhappy because I am single, I am unhappy because I don't have money, etc*.
If we look at scientific studies on happiness, this is somewhat true. Happiness and contentment correlate with certain things outside of us, like financial security or safety. But on the inside, our experience of unhappiness comes from a different place.

This practice is also a little bit different because it doesn't clearly fall into one of these three buckets of mind-body practice, balancing practice, introspective practice.

If you really want to, you can sort of do it in that third bucket is introspective practice. But I would say this is the kind of meditation that can be done a little bit more informally. As you were experiencing life and something negative arises, that is the best time to do this practice. So this is a more difficult and advanced practice.

###### Negativityaa Practice
Close your eyes, sit up straight, back straight, neck straight, head straight, and try to find any amount of negativity within you. It can be a physical sensation, a thought, or something within you that disturbs your peace. For the next ~1-5 minutes. look at where that negativity arises from. What is underneath it? I'm asking you to track back the origin of your experience of negativity. So what you'll find is that there's some thoughts at the top and underneath you'll find probably some kind of emotion. You'll find some kind of lust or anger or sadness or shame or guilt, and that this is sort of fueling the thoughts.

But I want you to look even further beneath that and ask yourself, where does this lust come from within me? And remember that the goal of this practice is not to come up with some sort of psychoanalytic formulation. We don't wanna look at things like our childhood or reasons. Steer clear of all reasons.

Where is this negativity within me? There are thoughts, and what is underneath those thoughts? There are emotions. What is underneath those emotions? Just keep digging under the source of negativity, look what's underneath that, underneath that, and underneath that, until you find what's at the bottom.

As you practice this diligently for an extended period of time, and especially combine it with other sorts of meditative practices, more formal focusing practices, breathing practices, you may be surprised by the answer.

The beautiful thing about this practice is that even your mind will rebel against it, because your mind will say, this doesn't work, or I've already figured this out, or I already know I am unhappy. *I'm unhappy because this thing happened in the past, or I'm alone, or this problem, or this problem, or this problem.* And even as you sit down to do the practice, your mind will serve you a plate of negativity about how useless this practice is to do. And look at where that comes from.

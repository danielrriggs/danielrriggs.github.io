Alias: Sankhya.

Dualistic orthodox school of Hindu philosophy.

[[Samkhya]] divides existence and individual being into two aspects: [[Purusha]] and [[Prakriti]]. Existence and the individual being arise when [[Purusha]] and [[Prakriti]] come together.

The two sides of Sankhya, [[Purusha]] and [[Prakriti]], are the source of all [[Creation]].

The [[Purusha]] implies the [[Drashta]] and [[Prakriti]] implies the [[Drishya]].
[[Purusha]] means subjective being and [[Prakriti]] means objective, external existence.

According to Samkhya philosophy, all objects are made of three [[Gunas]], or, "characteristics".

Individual souls, according to Samkhya philosophy, are **accountable** for all [[Manifestation]] and diversity, whereas [[Prakriti]], or Nature, is **responsible** for all [[Manifestation]] and diversity. They become subject to Nature’s influence and embodied by its reality when they come into contact with it. [[Prakriti]] is the **first cause** of the cosmos and an eternal reality. It is the unmanifest ([[Avyaktam]]), basic resource, the totality of the universal energy in its purest form. It has no cause, yet it is the source and cause of all consequences, as well as “the ultimate basis of the empirical cosmos.”

According to Samkhya Philosophy, the matter ([[Prakriti]]) by the force of the soul ([[Purusha]] ) becomes [[Manifestation|Manifested]] as a [[Human Being]].

The [[Atman]] is [[Purusha]] and is the basis of all, though, at the same time detached from everything. In its view [[Maya]] which keeps everything going is [[Prakriti]]. The cosmos is contained in 25 [[Tattwa|Tattva]]s - of which [[Prakriti|Prakrti]] is one.

###### Samkhya Philosophy & Science
Energy may be converted but not generated or destroyed, according to the First Law of Thermodynamics. Matter and energy are interchangeable, according to Einstein’s equation E=mc2. Matter stores energy and can be transformed back to energy. Both ancient philosophy and current science claim that the energy that grows a walnut and the walnut itself are same. Everything is two things at the same time.

The most important [[Karma]]s that we actually engage in are actually in our [[Mind]]. We take mental actions all the time that we're not really aware of, and those mental actions have mental consequences.

If we look at the studies on how meditation builds [[Willpower]], controls [[Desire|desires]] and suppresses [[Impulsivity|Impulse]], it is through this process of restraint.

Every time my mind wanders away, I use willpower to bring it back. This is like one rep. My [[Mind]], my [[Frontal lobes]] literally have gotten stronger at restraining one [[Impulsivity|Impulse]], and it feels exhausting. The more that my [[Frontal lobes]] bring my attention to the [[Prana|breath]], literally the more swole my frontal lobes are becoming. They are strengthening their connections to other parts of my brain. As our [[Frontal lobes]] become stronger, suddenly we are able to restrain our [[Impulsivity|Impulse]]s more easily. Then, I can start eating healthy, I can wake up on time, all kinds of other [[Desire|desires]] I used to fall victim to, like spending time on my phone, I will have more control over. This is a mental [[Karma]].

When we think, our thinking is very automatic. We don't actually control what we think. Yet, we assume that these thoughts are true, because they happen automatically. Let's say you went through a breakup. Your [[Mind]] could think things which you believe to be true, lile:
*"I spent the best years of my life on this person, all the good people are taken at this point because I'm 23. I've wasted so much time."*, etc.
But, you could also [[Cognitive reframing|Cognitively reframe]]:
"*I've learned something from this experience. Anything I do is an experience I can learn from.*", etc.

it's not that one is true and one is false. It's literally that we can **train** our [[Mind]] to start to think in a different way.

At the very beginning, my mind has an automatic thought of "I've wasted time". And then (maybe with the help of a therapist), we can spend a lot of energy to shift that thought to "even though I've spent a lot of time, I have learned something from this experience." Both statements can be true. The first time we do it, it requires a lot of energy. But then as we practice this, and we start thinking this way again, and again, and again, this eventually becomes our default experience. This becomes the thought that is automatically generated in our mind.

Think about the consequence of that. As you go through life, whatever setbacks you have, your automatically think, "I've learned alot, I'll be better next time". This is literally what we do in [[Psychotherapy]] when we teach people [[Cognitive reframing]]. Once they learn how to reframe, they start to live their lives with a different [[Mindset]].

All of this is a mental [[Karma]] - it's an action taken within the mind. The more that we take the right actions in the mind, the more we will cultivate the garden of our mind into something that we want it to be.

**How Neurons Wire**
The more that neurons wire in a particular way - the more we generate particular thoughts - the easier it is to generate that same thought again. The mind forms habitual thinking. This is all based on the [[Karma|karmic]] actions we take in our [[Mind]]. If we think in a particular way, that thinking will become more common.

In the very beginning, thinking in a different way is difficult. As we think in that way, more and more, that thinking will become more natural.

**Mental [[Karma]]s that have been elucidated in [[Yoga]]**
1) [[Attachment]]
When something happens to us, we tend to naturally have a thought about what this means for us - this is usually an [[Attachment]].

[[Dukkha|suffering]] comes from [[Attachment]] - the projections of the [[Mind]].

We don't know what other's judgments of us will be.

The [[Dukkha|suffering]] in our life, at least some percentage of it, it created by you - by the [[Attachment]]s our [[Mind]] puts onto the thing that is happening.

2) [[Vasana]] - habitual thinking.
A simple example is a song getting stuck in your head. We do not want Baby Shark stuck in our head. The mind has a certain automaticity to it.

The majority of your thoughts are just like songs stuck in your head. You don't think about what to think. Your mind naturally produces thoughts. Especially when it comes to [[Trauma]], we see this all the time.

What is a trigger? Triggers a what? Triggers a chain of thinking.

If I have a phobia of dogs and I see a dog, this triggers a [[Vasana]]. Now I go through thinking the same things over and over again.

You can see [[Vasana]]s in all kinds of places if you know what you're looking for. Anytime you engage in a political discussion with someone who has strongly held political beliefs, you will find the same [[Vasana]] over and over again. You say one triggering word and the rant begins. People are always thinking habitually.

Something beautiful can happen - it's a little bit advanced - but if you practice it, you'll get good at it. As you **notice** the [[Mind]] enter into a habitual habit, just **be aware** of it. Just say to yourself, "wow, this is the mental habit of my [[Mind]]". Technically, it's not even that you [[Desire|want]] the particular thing. Even the [[Desire]] itself can be a mental habit. Most of our [[Desire|desires]] are habitual in nature. Every day after work, I want to play a video game. I want to play it, I want to play it, I want to play it. This is a mental habit that we have.

As we notice, *"oh my God, most of my [[Mind]] is actually behaving on autopilot*" you notice that this is a mental habit outside of you. Most of the time when we have [[Vasana]]s, we think that this is me and that this is real. We don't observe the pattern of our thinking, we get caught up in our thinking.

Think again about this person who is politically very motivated. They don't really realize that their [[Mind]] is all warped by this level of political thinking.

As I started to really study the [[Vasana]]s of my mind, I discovered something quite shocking, which is 99% of my thinking is [[Vasana]]s.

[[Studying]] is programming a [[Vasana]]. We are trying to create a mental habit of thoughts that we can recall and access later.

First, observe that a lot of your thinking is habitual.
Second, as you notice a mental habit, ask yourself, do I want to continue this habit? Or do I want to change this habit?
How do you propagate a habit? And how do you extinguish a habit? The more that you engage in the habit, the more it will become habitual.

As you abstain from engaging in that mental habit, if you start to redirect your [[Mind]] in a different way (this is why we practice [[Meditation]] - so we can [[Dharana|focus]] our [[Mind]] in a different way), then the habit will start to dissolve.

3) [[Klishta]] - Associations with Objects
[[Dukkha|Suffering]] comes from the coloring of my [[Mind]].

The more insidious thing is that, as we develop more and more klishtas, our sense of [[Perception]] deviates more and more from [[Reality]]. This is what we see in [[Trauma]]. People who are [[Trauma|traumatized]] [[Attachment|attach]] all kinds of [[Meaning]]s to simple stimuli.

Ex: I texted someone and they didn't text back - *"This means they don't like me; I'm annoying them; they're hanging out with someone else, etc."* The real problem isn't the world out there, it's the flavoring their [[Mind]] adds to the experience of their life.

The process of uncoloring a klishta primarily happens with observation.
- In the [[Mentality Materiality Meditation]], we practice the skill of uncoloring our mind.

There is a common perception that the most important [[Karma]]s are the actions that we take - the actions I take, the words I say - but, in my experience, the most important [[Karma]]s are mental [[Karma]]s. If I can cultivate my mind in the right way, the way that I experience and live my life will be transformed.
